# Interaction spec

How the editor behaves. Visual tokens live in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## Layout

Nuzky starts on the home screen with every project; the editor of the last project is open behind it (see Home screen). The editor: top bar (Projects, name and save state in the middle, AI run, background jobs, AI, undo/redo, Export) · left panel (Media, Audio, Text, Captions, Transcript, Transitions, Filters) · preview with transport and Ratio · inspector · timeline across the bottom, its main track starting with the Cover tile · the AI panel, when open, as a column at the right edge by default. The layout follows CapCut so its users find everything where they expect it. Each control exists once: the canvas format lives in Ratio under the preview, the timecode in the transport.

The library, the inspector and the timeline start close to CapCut's proportions (28 % of the width each, 35 % of the height) and follow the window until resized. Drag the gap right of the library, left of the inspector or above the timeline to resize that pane (a grip shows while the pointer is over it); double-click resets it to its share of the window, and ←/→ (↑/↓ above the timeline) on the focused gap resize by 24 px. The preview always keeps at least 340 px of width and 300 px of height: a pane stops there, and in a smaller window the inspector gives way first, then the library; both get their size back when the window grows. The sizes are remembered.

## Response time

| Action | Feedback |
|---|---|
| Click, toggle, select | Visual state change in the same frame |
| Seek or scrub | Last frame stays on screen until the new one arrives, so the preview never flashes |
| Import | Item appears at once with a skeleton thumbnail; audio preparation shows a thin progress bar on the item |
| Import of video that decodes slowly (HEVC from phones, AV1, VP9, larger than 1080p) | A lighter copy for the preview is made in the background, one file at a time: "Preparing preview of IMG_0042.MOV 42%" with Stop in the top bar. Until it is ready the preview plays the original; from the next pause on it plays the copy, which has the same frames at the same times. Thumbnails made after that come from it too. Export always reads the original. A failed copy says so in a toast and the preview keeps the original |
| Export, captions, transcript | Background job with percentage and phase in the top bar; the user keeps editing; a toast reports the result |
| Sound library search, preview, add | Searching shows skeleton rows at once; a sound that has to download shows a spinner on its picture (preview) or the percentage on its + (add), and the panel stays usable |

## Flows

### Import
Entry: Import button, `Ctrl+I`, or files dropped on the window. Files dropped on the timeline are also placed on it; while media files are dragged over the timeline, an `accent` line shows where they would land. Each picked or dropped file shows at once in Media (and Audio for sound files) with its name and a skeleton thumbnail while it is read. The Audio tab's "Add music or sound", under In project, opens the picker filtered to audio.
Outcome: items appear in Media (all kinds) and, for audio, in Audio. Unsupported files produce one toast naming each file by name only with a short reason ("build.log is not a video, audio or image file"); the rest still import.
Hovering or focusing a media or audio item shows `+` (add at playhead), on videos and images a picture-in-picture button (see Add to timeline), and a trash button (remove the item and its clips at once; a toast offers Undo, like deleting clips).

### Add to timeline
`+` on a media item adds it at the playhead (video and images on the main track, audio on an audio track). Dragging an item onto a track places it at the pointer. The new clip is selected.

**Picture in picture**: the button on a video or image item puts it at the playhead in a small window over the main track, for a screenshot or a second camera beside a talking head. The window's longer side is 45 % of the canvas's shorter side; it sits in the top right corner, against the corner of the Reels and TikTok safe area on vertical videos and 4 % of the shorter side from the edges on the others, with corners rounded at 15 % and a soft shadow. It goes on the first overlay track free there, else on a new one above the others. A video plays its whole length, an image lasts 3 s. One undo step; the new clip is selected, so the inspector shows its crop and shape.

### Sound library
The Audio tab has three tabs: In project (the project's own sound files, with Add music or sound), Music and Sound effects. It opens on In project and remembers the last one. Music and Sound effects show only sounds under CC0 or CC BY, the licences that allow monetized videos; anything else a catalogue returns never shows.

Sound effects lists the effects built into Nuzky (CC0, they work offline) with chips for their kinds (Whoosh, Transition, Pop and click, Bell and alert, Impact, Funny, Everyday) that scroll with the list. Typing filters them at once and, after a short pause or Enter, also searches online; online results follow under Online. Music has nothing built in: it offers ideas (Upbeat, Chill…) and searches online only when the user types or picks one; nothing is sent before. Online search uses Openverse (its Freesound and Wikimedia Commons sounds; music is searched by its words, as almost no other music there is filed as music; Jamendo's tracks stay out while its commercial terms are unsettled), or for sound effects Freesound when the user saved their own API key under Where to search (the sliders button beside the field; `Esc` closes it and returns there) and turned it on; the switch stays disabled until a key is saved. Only the typed text is sent. The results end with "Search uses Openverse. Nuzky is not endorsed or certified by Openverse." and the advice to check a sound's page, since catalogue licences can be wrong; More results loads the next page.

A row shows the title, the author and where it comes from (or the kind for a built-in one), the licence (CC BY or CC0) and the length. Clicking a row (or its picture, the keyboard's way) plays the sound through the app's own audio, downloading it first when needed (a spinner meanwhile); the row stays highlighted while it plays. Clicking again, another sound, Play on the timeline or leaving the tab stops it, and the timeline pauses while it plays. The licence opens the sound's page at its source in the browser. `+` on hover or focus, or a double click, adds the sound at the playhead on an audio track as one undo step, downloading it first (the `+` shows the percentage; clicking it stops the download, a double click does not). Library sounds are added with `+` or a double click, not by dragging. The file goes to Nuzky's sound library beside the projects, and the asset remembers the source, author, licence and page. A sound the project already has shows a check and adds another clip of the same file. While the AI is editing, `+` is disabled with the reason; playing and searching still work.

When a CC BY sound plays on the timeline, the bottom of the Audio tab says how many need credit, with Copy credits: it copies, for the video's description, each CC BY sound's title, author, page, licence with its link and what the video changed ("Synced to video; trimmed, faded."), then the CC0 ones as a courtesy, and a toast says where to paste it. The project's own CC BY audio shows a CC BY chip. Export writes the same text beside the video (see Export).

Offline, or when Openverse limits the searches, the online part says so in place of results with Try again (in Sound effects one line under the built-in sounds, which still show). A failed download says why in a toast and adds nothing.

### Edit clips
- **Select**: click. `Shift`-click adds to the selection. Click empty space or press `Esc` to clear.
- **Keyboard**: the clips are one tab stop (the clip focused last, else the first selected, else the first on the main track). On a focused clip `←`/`→` go to the previous or next clip on its track and `↑`/`↓` to the nearest clip on the track above or below, without moving the playhead, and select the clip they reach, so `Delete`, `S`, `Q`, `W` and `Ctrl+D` act on the clip with focus. `Ctrl` with an arrow moves focus without selecting, to build a selection with `Shift+Enter` (adds or removes the focused clip); `Enter` selects only it. `Alt+←`/`Alt+→` nudge it a frame (`Shift`: a second), stopping at its neighbours; on the main track they trade places with the clip beside it. A burst of nudges is one undo step. The Menu key or `Shift+F10` opens the clip's context menu; closing the menu returns focus to the clip. When the focused clip is deleted, focus and selection move to the clip that took its place on the track, else the one before it. `Space` works as everywhere.
- **Move**: drag the clip body. A ghost follows the pointer and snaps within 8 px to the playhead and clip edges; a vertical line shows the snap. Dropping on an occupied spot creates a new track. `Esc` cancels the drag, and so does the system taking the pointer or the window losing focus.
- **Main track**: magnetic. Clips sit back to back; moving reorders, deleting closes the gap. While a clip is dragged the track shows the result live: the neighbours move aside, a dashed slot and an `accent` bar mark where it goes in, and the gap it left closes. Trimming a main-track clip keeps its start and moves the clips after it with the edge. Media dragged over the main track marks the cut it would go in at.
- **Trim**: drag a clip edge. A tooltip shows the new duration. The handle stops at the end of the source media, taking the clip's speed into account.
- **Fade**: a clip on an audio track has a dot in each top corner, shown while the pointer is over the clip or it is selected; while there is no fade it covers the top of the trim handle, so trimming takes the edge below it. Drag the left dot right to fade the sound in from the start, the right dot left to fade it out to the end, as in CapCut. The dot stays at the end of the fade, the clip shows the fade as it changes, and a tooltip its length ("Fade in 1.2 s"). Fades go in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, whichever is shorter, and each drag is one undo step. `Esc` cancels the drag and leaves the fade as it was. The inspector's Audio tab sets the same fades from the keyboard, and the fades of a video clip's own sound. While the AI is editing, the dots are hidden.
- **Split**: at the playhead. `S` and the Split button split the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it, and are disabled with the reason when there is none; the context menu splits the selected clips under the playhead.
- **Cuts sound clean**: where two pieces of sound meet that do not play on from each other, they crossfade over 20 ms around the cut (10 ms on each side, reaching past the clips only into quiet sound of the files), so no cut clicks or dips; where the sound past the cut is not quiet, such as the start of a deleted word, the two sides fade out and in over 5 ms instead, so nothing deleted is heard. Split halves that play on, and cuts with a transition, are left as they are.
- **Delete left / right**: `Q` and `W` or the buttons next to Split remove the part of the clip left or right of the playhead, CapCut's fast way to cut a talking head. They act on the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it. On the main track the time is cut from every track, so captions, titles and overlays stay in sync; audio tracks set to keep in place (see Transcript) are left alone. A clip on another track is trimmed instead. After `Q` the playhead sits on the cut. Each press is one undo step.
- **Duplicate**: `Ctrl+D`, the Duplicate button or the context menu. The copy goes right after the original and is selected.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace`, the toolbar button or the context menu; all of them show a toast with Undo. With a transition selected they remove the transition.
- **Context menu**: right-click a clip for Split, Duplicate, Detach audio, Select all on track and Delete. Right-clicking a clip outside the selection selects it first; every item then acts on the selection, and with several clips selected the labels say how many (“Delete 3 clips”). Select all on track uses the clicked clip's track. Unavailable items are disabled with the reason in a tooltip; ↑/↓ reach them too and show the reason. ↑/↓ move, Esc closes.
- **Undo/redo**: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y`. A slider drag, a typing burst, a preview drag or a caption restyle is one step. Undo in a toast undoes the step the toast names; once another change follows it, the toast no longer offers Undo.
- **Versions**: the clock button after Undo and Redo lists the newest 20 versions of the project, newest first: what made each (an AI run's label with a sparkle, or the step, such as Split, Undo or Restore) and when. A version is the project after each kept AI run, each of your steps, each undo, redo and restore, and as it was opened. They stay after a restart, up to the newest 200. The current one has a check. Choosing another restores it as one step, and a toast offers Undo; the versions after it stay in the list. `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` restores, `Esc` closes. While the AI is editing, the button is disabled with the reason.
- **Order**: edits, undo and redo apply one at a time in the order they were made. An edit made before the previous one is confirmed builds on it, so two quick changes (two sliders, two keyframes) both stick.

### Timeline display
Clips are drawn 1 px in from each side so every cut is visible. Video clips show a filmstrip whose frames match the source position under each tile, speed included (a single repeated thumbnail until the filmstrip is ready), and a thin waveform strip at the bottom while they have sound. A waveform draws one bar per pixel of what is on screen. While a file's sound is still being prepared after import, its waveform fills in from the start as the sound is decoded; parts not decoded yet stay empty. Clips not at 1x carry a speed badge ("2x"). Thin bars at the top edge show the length of the entry (left) and exit (right) animation. A sound fade on an audio clip shows as a line from the bottom corner up to where the fade ends, with the corner above it shaded. The selected clip shows its keyframes as diamonds; clicking one, or `Enter` on a focused one, moves the playhead there. Caption clips show only their text. When sound runs past the last picture or text, the video ends there: a dashed line on the ruler and lanes marks the end, the time after it is dimmed, and the ruler's tooltip says the sound after it is not exported.

### Transitions
Every cut on the main track has a small square. Clicking an empty one adds a 0.5 s Dissolve and selects it; clicking a set one selects it. A selected transition opens in the inspector with its name, duration and Remove; the kind is picked in the Transitions tab. The Transitions tab applies to the selected cut, else the cut before the selected main-track clip, else the cut nearest the playhead, and names that cut. Duration is capped at 2 s and by the shorter neighbouring clip. None removes the transition.

### Filters
The Filters tab applies a preset (None, Vivid, Warm, Cool, Mono, Fade, Moody, Punch) to the selected video or image clip. A preset replaces the clip's Adjust values; the matching preset is marked. Without a media clip selected the tiles are disabled with the reason.

### Playback
`Space` plays and pauses; the focused control keeps focus and is not pressed. A focused checkbox or menu item keeps Space for itself. Clicking or dragging the ruler scrubs. `←`/`→` step one frame, with `Shift` one second. `Home`/`End` jump to the ends. A focused slider keeps its own arrow, Home and End keys. The sound card drives the clock, so picture and sound stay in sync.

### Preview
Clicking the paused preview selects the top-most visible layer under the pointer, so a click outside the curve of a circle or a rounded corner reaches the layer below; clicking where no layer is clears the selection. The selected layer gets a box: drag inside it to move, drag a corner to scale uniformly, drag the round handle to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°). On videos and images, dragging the bar in the middle of an edge crops that edge: the rest of the picture stays where it is, a dashed outline shows the whole picture while dragging, and each edge stops 5 % short of the opposite one. An edge too short on screen for its bar to clear the corner handles has none; the inspector crops it. The box of a layer larger than the frame is cut off at the edge of the preview area, and handles that would leave it stay on that edge, so they can always be grabbed. While moving, the layer centre snaps to the canvas centre lines within 6 px and a guide shows. Each gesture is one undo step. If the clip has keyframes, the gesture writes the keyframe at the playhead. Ratio, right of the transport, switches the canvas between 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 and 4:5; in its menu ↑/↓ move, and choosing or Esc returns focus to Ratio.

### Inspector
Shows the selected clip with CapCut's tabs, remembering the last tab per clip kind:

| Selection | Tabs |
|---|---|
| Video clip | Video (transform, crop and shape, background, motion), Adjust, Speed, Animation, Audio (only when the file has sound) |
| Image clip | Image (transform, crop and shape, background, motion), Adjust, Animation |
| Text or caption clip | Text, Animation, Transform |
| Audio clip (also detached sound) | Audio: volume, fades, Clean voice, Lower under speech, speed |
| Transition | Kind name, duration, Remove |
| Nothing | Project: format (opens Ratio) and frame rate, background colour or blur with strength |
| Several clips | Duplicate, Delete, and the controls they all share: Transform, Font (text), Background and Adjust (video, images), Audio volume, Clean voice and Lower under speech (clips with sound) |

Adjust offers Exposure, Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Saturation, Temperature, Tint, Fade, and Vignette for single or multiple media clips.

While playing, values that follow keyframes update ten times a second; paused or scrubbing, they show the exact frame. Number fields keep what you type until Enter or blur, so "-" or "1," is never rejected mid-typing; Esc restores the value, ↑/↓ step (`Shift` ×10), commas work as decimal points. Changes preview live.

- **Keyframes**: the diamond in the Transform header adds a keyframe with the current values at the playhead, or removes the one there (filled diamond). Arrows jump to the previous and next keyframe. Once a clip has keyframes, every transform change updates or creates the keyframe at the playhead. A keyframe moves on to the next one at an even pace, or on a smooth curve when Motion wrote it; a new keyframe takes the curve of the one before it. Removing the last keyframe keeps its values. Reset clears the transform and all keyframes and keeps the crop at the playhead.
- **Crop and shape** (videos and images), right under Transform: Shape is Rectangle, Rounded (Corners from 1 to 99 % of half the shorter side, 15 % to start with, as a new picture in picture has) or Circle. Circle rounds fully and crops the longer side so a square is left, in the same undo step; the layer moves so the side nearer the canvas edge stays put, so a picture in picture in a corner stays in the corner and a centred layer stays centred. Crop left, right, top and bottom cut that share of the picture off, each stopping 5 % short of the opposite edge; the rest stays where it is. The crop is part of the transform: once the clip has keyframes, a crop change writes the keyframe at the playhead like the other transform values, so a crop can move, and a line says so. Border draws up to 40 px (canvas pixels) outside the edge in its color; Border color shows once it is wider than 0. Shadow puts a soft shadow under the layer, 0–100 %. Reset crop and shape shows the whole picture with plain edges again, every keyframe included. The preview and the export draw the same edge.
- **Background** (videos and images), below Crop and shape: None, Blur, Color or Image behind the person, like CapCut's background removal. Blur blurs the clip's own picture behind the person, Strength 5–100 % (50 % to start with), with the person left out of the blur, so the room fills in behind them instead of a halo of their colours; it is graded with the clip, Adjust included. Color (a dark grey to start with) and Image (an image of the project, covering the whole clip, so a crop or its move does not move it) show as picked, Adjust does not recolour them. Image takes the newest image of the project (never the clip's own picture), or asks for one to import when there is none; the image button, with a small picture of the image, lists the project's images and Import image…. Choosing the option already on keeps its strength, colour or image. The person is found once per file, in the frames the clips show, as a job, one file at a time: meanwhile "Finding the person · 42%" with a bar shows under the controls ("Waiting to find the person" while another file goes first), the top bar lists the job, and the preview shows those clips as recorded, switching part by part as the job gets through them, even while paused. A minute of 1080p video at 30 fps takes about 12 s, of 4K at 60 fps about 30 s. Export always has the background: it finds the person first when the job has not. Split, trimmed and copied clips of the file share what was found; another file, or the file changed, is looked at again. Choosing None, removing the clip or opening another project stops a job no clip needs any more; what it found stays. When the person model is missing a row says that showing and exporting the background needs it and offers to download it (1 MB); when the models cannot run on this computer the row says why and the options stay disabled. When the person cannot be found in a file, a toast and the row say why: the clip shows as recorded and None lets it export. The edge is a few pixels soft and follows the person from frame to frame without flickering; it suits one person filling much of the frame, as in a talking head. With several clips selected, a dash means they differ; a choice applies to all of them as one undo step.
- **Motion**: a slow camera move on a video or image clip in one click, below Background. Push in zooms in, Pull out starts zoomed in and ends on the clip's own framing, Ken Burns zooms in while drifting right; None removes the motion. A new move runs over the whole clip at the strength used last (10 % at first). Strength (6, 10 or 15 %) and Range rewrite the clip's move and are disabled with the reason until it has one. Range is the whole clip, or Rest of sentence: from the playhead to the end of the sentence said there, found in the transcript and kept inside the clip; without a transcript, or with the playhead off the clip, a toast says why and nothing changes. Picking Rest of sentence again moves the move to the sentence at the playhead. Another preset keeps the range of the move it replaces. The move starts and ends gently and is written as two ordinary keyframes, so Transform edits it like any keyframes, and each click is one undo step. On vertical videos it zooms about the middle of the Reels and TikTok safe area, otherwise about the centre of the frame, so a face framed there stays put and clear of the apps' buttons. The section marks the clip's move with its strength and range; keyframes the user changed by hand mark no preset. A preset on a clip with keyframes of its own replaces them, starting from the picture at the playhead, and None removes them keeping that picture; both show a toast that offers Undo. The tiles play their move while hovered or focused.
- **Several clips**: one change applies to every selected clip as one undo step, a slider drag included. Where the clips differ the field shows "—"; a typed value then applies to all. A transform change applies to the whole clip, every keyframe included, so "110 % on every clip" is Select all on track plus one value.
- **Speed**: presets 0.5x–3x or a logarithmic slider from 0.1x to 10x. Speed changes the clip length; the tab shows the new duration and the source length used. Keep pitch, a checkbox shown when the clip has sound and is not at 1x, keeps a voice as high as at 1x instead of higher when faster and lower when slower. Moving a clip off 1x turns it on; turned off, it stays off until the clip is back at 1x. Clips sped up in a project from before it keep the changed pitch until it is checked. Playback and export sound the same. Captions, word times and cuts stay where speed puts them, the sound stays within 11 ms of there, and sound cut away does not come back. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut.
- **Animation**: In and Out each take one preset with a duration; the selected preset is marked and the slot label shows a dot when set.
- **Audio**: volume, fade in and fade out (in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, like the dots on the timeline), Clean voice, Lower under speech. Detach audio moves the sound to its own audio track and silences the video clip; disabled with the reason when there is nothing to detach.
- **Clean voice**: a checkbox for speech recorded on a phone, off by default. A high-pass at 80 Hz takes away rumble and 50/60 Hz hum, gentle noise reduction (RNNoise with 15 % of the original mixed back, so speech does not sound processed) lowers steady room noise by about 15 dB between words, and a de-esser softens harsh s sounds by up to 6 dB. Each file's cleaned sound is prepared once in the background: meanwhile "Cleaning voice · 42%" with a bar shows under the checkbox, the top bar lists the job, and playback keeps the original sound, then switches to the cleaned one without stopping. Export always has the cleaned sound; it waits for the preparation or does it itself. Turning it off, removing the clip or opening another project stops a preparation that no clip needs any more. Cuts, crossfades and word times stay where they are; the waveform shows the original sound. A split whose halves differ in Clean voice crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have it; checking turns it on for all of them, as one undo step.
- **Lower under speech** (ducking): a checkbox for music under a talking head, off by default. Checked, the clip goes 12 dB down while someone speaks and comes back in the pauses; Lower by, shown under it, sets 1–40 dB. Speech is the sound of video clips, also sound detached from them, on unmuted tracks: any 10 ms of their file louder than -40 dBFS, so other loud sound in a video counts too, while imported audio files never do and a clip that lowers itself never lowers another. The clip starts going down 150 ms before the first word, so the word is clear, stays down through gaps between words up to 250 ms and comes back over 400 ms, without clicks. Playback and export sound the same, Reels levelling to -14 LUFS included; projects without it sound exactly as before. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have it; checking turns it on at 12 dB for the others and keeps the strength of those that have it, as one undo step, and Lower by shows once all have it.

### Text and captions
Text presets add a text clip at the playhead, centred in the frame, and select it; captions sit a little below the middle (y +0.15, where reels put them). Transform Reset puts each back there. The inspector offers the same style presets under the same names as the Text tab, or the Captions tab for a caption. The inspector calls a caption clip "Caption".

**Font**: the Font field opens a searchable list. Fonts that ship with Nuzky come first, each name drawn in its own face; fonts installed on the computer follow. ↑/↓ move, Enter picks, Esc closes. Text without a font uses Inter, also on machines without it. A font the computer does not have shows a warning; the engine draws Inter instead. Presets carry no font, so the font survives a preset change.

**Captions**: choose language, accuracy, words per caption (1, 2, 3 or Phrases; 1–3 words also stay within 15 characters), style, key words and font, then Generate. Reel is the default style: Inter 95 px regular, white with a 7.5 px black outline, no box, 2 words. On vertical videos captions keep to the Instagram Reels and TikTok safe area (clear of the top bar, the like and comment rail and the caption and buttons at the bottom; 1080×1920: 250 px top, 180 px right, 500 px bottom, 60 px left): longer captions wrap instead of reaching under the app's interface, and restyling keeps that width. Captions keep a number with the word before it ("iPhone 17") and a sentence does not end on a lone word when the caption before can give one. A caption never runs across a cut. Cutting speech after the captions were made takes the cut words out of them: a caption with words left on both sides of a cut becomes two, one with no word left goes, and a caption whose text was typed over keeps its longer side whole. The first run downloads the model with progress. Recognition runs in the background and can be cancelled; while it runs the fields are locked and a starting job shows an indeterminate bar. Captions and Transcript share the language and accuracy and one recognition at a time. The language starts as the computer's language when it is offered (else Detect automatically) and is remembered once picked. Generating again replaces the previous Captions track, keeps the look of the captions there (style, font, animations and key words), and the button label says so. With captions on the timeline, picking a style, key words or a font changes every caption at once, as one undo step; words per caption apply when you regenerate. Before there are captions, the choices wait for Generate.

**Caption styles**: a style is a whole look for reels: size, colours, outline or box, the entry and exit animation of every caption, a karaoke highlight and key words, and for some a font from the bundled fonts. Picking one applies all of it to every caption; a style without its own font keeps the font the captions have, so a font picked separately survives it. The style matching the captions is checked. A caption's inspector offers the same styles for that caption alone, animations included. Agents pick the same styles by name (`build_captions` `style_preset`).

**My styles**: + under Style saves the current look (the selected caption's, else the first caption's, else the look waiting for Generate) under a name typed in place; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels. A saved style is a tile like the built-in ones and applies the same way. Its … button on hover, a right click, or the context menu key on a focused tile offers Rename (also `F2`) and Delete; Delete shows a toast with Undo. Names are unique and never a built-in style's name; a refused name says why under the field, which stays open. Focus returns to the tile, or to + when there is none. Saving, renaming and deleting work while recognition runs, as they do not change the project; the AI lock stops them like every change. Generate captions stays in view under the choices. My styles are kept beside the projects, not in a project, so every project and an agent (`style_preset` by name) can use them, also after a restart.

**Key words**: Off, Emphasis, First, Last or Longest, with their colour. Emphasis colours the numbers and the words said louder than the rest, scored like the punch-in suggestions (`analyze(kind: "emphasis")`): how much louder than the median word, an exclamation, and a number; where a word sits in its sentence does not count, so some captions have none and key words lie at least 1.5 s apart. Words shorter than four letters are never key unless they are numbers. The words are scored once when the captions are made, from their sound (a file whose sound cannot be read counts numbers and exclamations only), so they stay key through a word correction, a split, a trim or a cut, and regenerating scores them again. First, Last and Longest mark that word of every caption instead. Like karaoke, a caption whose words were added or removed by hand colours none. The word being spoken takes the karaoke highlight over its key word colour, so the two combine. A caption's inspector offers Key words for that caption.

Saved style names are trimmed, contain 1–40 characters and are unique across saved and built-in packages ignoring case and treating underscores and hyphens as spaces. An unreadable or damaged styles file reports an error and is never overwritten when another style is saved.

**Karaoke**: the Karaoke style (Reel with the spoken word in yellow), Green box (bold white on a dark box, the spoken word green), Spotlight and White box light up the word being said. Each generated caption keeps when each of its words is heard; a word is drawn in the highlight colour from its start until its end, and nothing is lit between words, before the first or while the caption holds after the last. Only the colour changes, so the size and the line breaks stay put. Split, trim, `Q`/`W`, text cuts and Duplicate keep every word on its moment. Fixing words in a caption's text keeps the timing as long as it has as many words as before (a corrected typo lights up when the old word did); adding or removing a word turns the caption's highlight off, the inspector says so under Highlight colour, and regenerating brings it back. A caption's inspector offers Highlight spoken word with its colour.

### Transcript
Text-based editing, as in CapCut. Transcribe timeline recognises each video file whose sound plays on the timeline (music and other audio files are not listened to), once and in the file's own time; a captions run does the same and reuses what is already recognised. The transcript belongs to the file, so it follows every edit (cuts, trims, `Q`/`W`, speed, detached sound, mute, undo and redo), is still there after a restart and is reused when the file is in another project. The refresh button recognises every file again. After recognising Czech or English, the job measures where each word starts and ends in the sound ("Measuring word times"), so cuts in speech without pauses land between words; the first time, it downloads that language's word timing model with progress ("Downloading word timing model"). Without the model, for example offline, the transcript still comes, with word times estimated as before, and the toast says the word times are estimated. Cancel stops it within a second. When a clip's file has no transcript yet, a note says "1 clip not transcribed" with Transcribe; until then Delete, Remove pauses and Remove retakes and pauses are disabled with that reason. The words flow as text in paragraphs that break where speech pauses for a second or more, each led by its timecode. Pauses longer than the threshold (0.5 s by default) show as chips with their length. While playing, the current word is highlighted and kept in view.

- **Select**: click a word to jump there and select it; drag or `Shift`-click to extend. With the text focused, `←`/`→` move word by word (`Shift` extends), `Home`/`End` jump to the ends, `Enter` jumps to the selection, `Esc` clears it, `Space` plays without leaving the text.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace` or the Delete button in the bar under the text, which shows the selection's word count and length. It cuts the selected words and the silence around them, keeping 0.12 s after the word before and 0.08 s before the word after, counted from where those words are heard in the sound, so a cut never clips a syllable, out of every track except audio kept in place, so captions and overlays stay in sync, as one undo step. A selection of pause chips alone removes those pauses. The playhead lands on the cut; a toast with the time removed offers Undo. While the bar shows, toasts sit above it so they never cover Delete. Inside the text these keys never delete timeline clips.
- **Remove pauses**: the button names the count and the time it removes ("Remove 5 pauses · 6.7 s") and cuts every pause chip at once. A pause between words keeps half the threshold next to each word, so a 2 s pause at 0.5 s becomes 0.5 s (where the next clip starts speaking at once, the whole pause stays after the last word of the clip before, so no sliver of a clip is left); silence longer than the threshold before the first word and after the last one is trimmed to 0.08 s and 0.12 s. Pauses and text cuts only lie inside clips that carry speech: clips in which words were recognised. A clip without words, such as B-roll, is never part of a pause and never cut from the text.
- **Remove retakes and pauses**: the prominent button under the pause row cleans up a talking head in one step, as an agent's rough cut does. It cuts the earlier attempts of every restarted sentence, keeping the last complete one, attempts that trail off before a new sentence starts, filler words that open a sentence ("ehm", "prostě") and every pause chip, as one undo step. Two sentences that differ in a negation, a number or a content word are not taken for attempts at one, and both stay. Words go with the silence around them as with Delete, pauses shrink as with Remove pauses, and the captions follow. A toast with Undo names what went and the time removed ("Removed 3 retakes, 2 filler words and pauses over 0.5 s (12.6 s)"). The button is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed and when there is nothing to remove.
- **Correct a word**: double-click a word, or press `F2` with one word selected, and type what was said; `Enter` or clicking elsewhere saves, `Esc` cancels. The word then reads that way in the transcript, in the captions that show it (their style and timing stay) and in captions generated again, also after a restart. The correction belongs to the project and to that recognised word: if the file is recognised again and the word comes out differently, it no longer applies. Typing the recognised text again removes it. A corrected word is underlined with dots and its tooltip says what was recognised. A toast offers Undo; one undo takes back the word and its captions. While AI is editing, a word cannot be corrected and the notice says why.
- **Suggest zooms**: the button next to the word count marks the sentences said with emphasis for a subtle punch-in: louder than the median sentence, an exclamation, or the opening hook; 1.2–8 s and three words or more, at least 5 s apart and about one per 12 s of timeline, each with its zoom (1.15–1.3×, more for a sentence that stands out more). A bar above the text says how many, with Dismiss and Apply N zooms. Apply takes focus when the suggestions arrive, `Esc` in the bar dismisses them, and focus then returns to Suggest zooms. Apply zooms in on every marked sentence as one undo step and a toast with the count offers Undo: main-track clips are split in the silence before and after the sentence, midway into it and at most 0.15 s from the words. When only silence lies between that point and a cut, or the cut is less than 0.3 s away, the zoom reaches the cut instead, so every piece of a take keeps words and its pauses can still be removed. The pieces in between scale up by the factor, keeping their position and rotation. The sound does not change. Clips with keyframes keep their own motion, and the toast says how many did. The suggestions go when the speech changes. Suggest zooms is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed or recognition runs, Apply while clips are not transcribed, and both, like every editing control, while the AI is editing.
- **Keep in place while cutting**: one checkbox per audio track, saved with the project and undoable. Tracks made for music and sound files start checked and keep playing across cuts; sound detached from a video starts unchecked, so it is cut with the picture. The setting also applies to `Q`/`W`.

### Export
`Ctrl+E` or Export opens a dialog: preset (Custom, Reels & TikTok), resolution (720p, 1080p, 1440p, 4K, with the resulting pixel size for the canvas), frame rate (24–60, default the project's), quality (High, Recommended, Smaller file) with an estimated file size. Reels & TikTok sets 1080p, 30 fps and Recommended and says "Loudness −14 LUFS, applied to the file": the export writes 1080×1920 H.264 High with AAC 48 kHz stereo and levels the sound to −14 LUFS integrated with true peak at most −1 dBTP (ITU-R BS.1770), while the project and the preview keep their own levels. Quality can change within the preset; another resolution or frame rate switches back to Custom. On a canvas that is not 9:16 the preset says to switch Ratio to 9:16 and Export… is disabled with that reason; no hidden crop or stretch. Preset, resolution and quality are remembered; a remembered Reels preset comes back only on a 9:16 project. A Reels export first measures the loudness (the dialog's progress says "Measuring loudness" for the first few percent), then renders; Cancel stops either phase within a second. Export… asks where to save and adds `.mp4` when the name has no extension. An existing file is only replaced once someone said so: the save dialog asks for the name it returns, and when the final name differs (`.mp4` was added) or the file appears while rendering, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?” with Choose another name… and Replace. Tab stays inside the dialog. It can be closed at any time, with `Esc` too unless an export is running (then Keep editing or Cancel export says what happens to it), and focus returns to what opened it; a running export shows its percentage in the top bar and clicking it reopens the dialog. Progress shows percentage and time left, with Cancel. When CC BY sounds from the sound library play in the video, export also writes their credits beside it as `<name>.credits.txt` (`reel.mp4` gets `reel.credits.txt`), and an existing credits file is replaced only once someone said so for that file: the dialog asks “reel.credits.txt already exists. Replace it?” on its own, since replacing the video is not consent to replace notes kept under that name. Success offers Show in folder, in the dialog or, when it is closed, in a toast. Failure shows the reason and Retry (same file); with the dialog closed a toast offers Details. Export is disabled with a reason while the timeline is empty.

Under the title, Video and Cover choose what to export; the dialog opens on Cover in the cover editor (`Ctrl+E` there too). Cover offers the format (9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube, with its size) and the file: PNG, or JPEG at the best quality under 2 MB, YouTube's limit. PNG is the default for the cover and JPEG for the YouTube thumbnail; the choice is remembered per format. Export… asks where to save in Pictures, named after the project ("Morning routine cover.png", "Morning routine YouTube thumbnail.jpg"), adds the extension when missing, and replaces an existing file only as the video does: the save dialog asks, and when the final name differs or the file appears meanwhile, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?”. The export is a job (Rendering, Finding the person when the cover needs it) with Cancel; it writes through a temporary file, so a cancelled or failed export leaves nothing behind. A format without a cover says so and offers Make the cover, which opens the cover editor on it. Success offers Show in folder; with the dialog closed a toast reports the result.

### Covers
A 9:16 cover for Reels and TikTok (1080 × 1920) and a YouTube thumbnail (1280 × 720), one of each per project, made from a frame of the video with text that can go behind the person. The Cover tile at the start of the main track, as in CapCut, opens the cover editor: it shows the 9:16 cover as it exports (else the YouTube one), or an empty shape until there is one, and is disabled with the reason while the timeline is empty. Clicking it again, Done or `Esc` (the first `Esc` clears what is selected on the cover) closes the editor; the playhead and the clip selection come back as they were.

- **The editor**: the preview shows the cover in place of the video, drawn by the engine exactly as it exports, and the transport becomes the cover bar: the frame's time, 9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube, what the apps cover, and Done. The library's place, titled Frames, holds Pick for me and the frames it found (the seven tabs act on the video, so they rest), the inspector the cover's settings, and the timeline chooses the frame: clips cannot be selected, moved or trimmed there, and nothing plays. Switching the format only changes what is shown.
- **The frame**: the playhead is the cover's frame. Clicking or dragging on the timeline, `←`/`→` (`Shift`: a second), `Home` and `End` choose it, one undo step per gesture or burst of keys; a frame changed by Undo, a candidate or an agent moves the playhead there. The frame time follows cuts as the engine keeps it on the same picture; a frame cut out of the video says so and asks for another.
- **Pick for me**: a job that looks through the whole video (Looking through the video, then Scoring frames) and cuts the person out of the best frame (Finding the person), with its phase, percentage and Stop. The first time it downloads the cover models (196 MB) in the same job, as the line under the button says; Stop works in every phase. It sets the cover to the best frame as one undo step, making the cover when there is none; a frame chosen while it ran is kept and a line says so. The frames it found (6 to 8, fewer in a short video, at least 2 s apart, best first) show as tiles with their time and why in plain words ("Sharp, well framed, facing you"), their score out of 100 in the tooltip, and as dots on the ruler with the same tooltip. Frames that score under 40 % of the best, such as a blink beside open eyes, are left out. They serve both formats; Pick for me, then Pick again, scores the framing of the format shown. Before the first pick the pane says what Pick for me finds and that clicking the timeline chooses any frame. The grid is one Tab stop: the arrows move, `Enter` or `Space` chooses, which sets the frame and moves the playhead. Without a face in the video a line says the frames are the sharpest and best lit instead. Where faces cannot be found on this computer, Pick for me is disabled with the reason; choosing a frame and text in front still work.
- **Before there is a cover**: the editor shows the cover it would make at the playhead with "Not made yet" and Use this frame; nothing is saved until the first change (a frame, a text, a setting), which makes it as one undo step. A format without a cover, while the other has one, offers Start from the 9:16 cover (or the YouTube thumbnail): the same frame, outline and texts, their sizes scaled to the format, as one step. A YouTube thumbnail of a vertical video is laid out as agents lay it out: the frame 1.4 times larger on the left third with its top at the top, so the cut of the shoulders falls below the bottom edge, and the texts beside the person over the other two thirds, in front of it; with the cover models installed, the frame blurred and dimmed fills the rest and a white 10 px line goes round the person.
- **Text**: the Text section's tiles (Behind head, Yellow box, Outline, the looks agents use) add "YOUR HOOK" in the upper part where the apps leave it free and select it. Texts are listed below, with Behind and a warning where the person covers too much of one; `Enter` on a row selects it. Click a text on the cover to select it, drag it to move, a corner to scale, the round handle to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°), with the centre snapping as in the preview; each gesture is one undo step. A selected text has the inspector's Text and Transform tabs: the text itself (a burst of typing is one step), the looks (a look keeps the text's font, like text presets), Behind person, then the style as for clips. Delete and Duplicate are in its header; `Delete` removes it with Undo in a toast.
- **The person**: Behind person draws the text behind them, Outline draws a line around them (colour, 1–40 px), and the Background is the frame (Blur, Dim) or a colour. Each needs the person cut out of the frame, which happens on its own as a job a moment after the frame stops changing; meanwhile the cover shows the whole frame with the text in front and a chip on the cover says "Finding the person" with the percentage and Stop. Without the cover models these settings are disabled with the reason and Download (196 MB) offers them; where they cannot run, they say so. When the person covers more than 30 % of a text behind them, a `warn` triangle marks the text on the cover and in the list, and the inspector says how much and to move it up or make it smaller. When nothing stands out in a frame, the text stays readable in front of what is there.
- **The frame's own place**: clicking the picture selects the frame; it moves and scales like a text, and the inspector's Frame section sets its scale, position and rotation, with Reset.
- **What the apps cover**: on by default and remembered, separately from the preview's safe zone; on a cover the Reels interface (as the preview's safe zone) and the 3:4 middle of the profile grid, on a YouTube thumbnail the corner of the duration badge. Remove cover (or thumbnail) removes it with Undo in a toast.
- **Media missing**: the cover says which file is missing and to put it back; Pick for me and the export say so too.
- **A damaged model**: when a cover model does not load, the job says so and Download again checks every cover model against its checksum and downloads the damaged ones again.
- **Make it with AI** in the editor's header, and Make a thumbnail in the AI panel, start the agent on a cover (see AI panel).

Above the field, chips show what goes with the message: the selected clips with their time range, and the playhead (whole seconds, so playing does not redraw them); × leaves one out of this message. Send is disabled with the reason while an answer runs, while another agent edits, or when the agent is not installed.

### Your style
Your style in the home screen's sidebar, under All projects, shows the style the AI follows when it edits: the file `EDIT.md`, which every agent connected to Nuzky reads as it is. Its count, in `accent`, is how many suggestions wait; without any it shows no count. Nothing of it leaves this computer.

- **Suggestions**: what learning found that is new or would change a rule of the style, each with what it says, the change (“Longest pause: 120 ms → 400 ms”), the confidence (High in 3 or more videos, Medium in 2, Low in 1) with in how many of the videos learned from and at how many moments it holds, and Show moments: each moment as the video, the time and what happened there (four, then Show all). Accept puts the rule into the style, Reject leaves it out; with more than one, Accept all takes every one. A suggestion changes nothing until it is accepted, and the same rule learned again with numbers within 15 % is no news.
- **Talk it through with AI**: AI in the top bar (`Ctrl+J`), or Talk it through with AI above Your rules, opens the AI panel beside the page; Close or `Ctrl+J` puts it away. It is the same agent and conversation as in the editor, but what is sent from here is about the style, not the open video (the field shows Your style instead of the clip and playhead chips). The agent reads the style, talks the change through, and once you agree makes it: your words become one of Your rules, or it changes, accepts or rejects a rule. The page shows the change at once, without reloading: the changed rule lights up for a moment and a toast says “The AI changed your style: …” with Undo. Every change it makes is a version starting with AI:. Only you can go back to the default style.
- **Your rules**: “Tell the AI what to do or not to do” takes a rule in plain words; `Enter` adds it. A rule's row changes it in place (the pencil, `F2` or `Enter` on the focused row; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels) or removes it (the bin or `Delete`, with Undo in the toast). They stand in the style under Your rules, before everything learned, and the agent is told they win over everything else. Learning never changes them.
- **Learned rules**: each rule in the style with what it tells the AI and how well the videos learned from back it now (or Not seen lately). A rule changed by hand says Changed by you, and learning leaves it as it is. Its … menu Reverts it to how the style had it before its last change or Removes it, both with Undo in a toast.
- **Not learned** (closed at first): rules the creator rejected, removed or reverted, which learning leaves alone, each with Learn again.
- **Learned from**: the videos the suggestions come from, newest first: a recording and the finished video cut from it with their match, or a project with when it was learned. The newest 12 count.
- **Learn my style…** opens a dialog with two tabs; the arrow keys move between them. **Videos** takes 1 to 3 pairs of a recording and the finished video cut from it (Add a pair, the ✕ removes one). Learn compares them one after another on this computer the way `nuzky style learn` does, so the same files give the same rules: each row shows its phase and percentage, then Matched with the share of the finished video's speech found in the recording, or why it was skipped (a finished video not cut from its recording). Review shows the suggestions. **Projects (.otio)** takes timelines cut in another editor and exported as OpenTimelineIO, which DaVinci Resolve and other editors write: Choose projects… (any number, Add projects for more, the ✕ removes one) reads each at once and says what it will teach (each recording whose sound it plays, with its clips, the length and the titles) and what is missing: a recording not on this computer, a sound file, and what the file holds that Nuzky does not read (effects, nested sequences, media on the web, which is never fetched). Learn recognises the speech of each recording the way `nuzky style learn --from` does; each row shows its phase over a progress bar, with the percentage once it is known, then Learned or why nothing was. When it ends, the dialog shows `EDIT.md` as learned, with Your rules kept. Nothing changes until Use as my style writes it and keeps what was learned; Learn from more goes back to the projects. Over an existing style the button is Replace my style…, which asks first, focused on Cancel (`Esc` answers it too): the learned rules, also those changed by hand, give way, Your rules stay. Either way a toast offers Undo and Versions keep the style before. When the style changed since the preview, anywhere, it is not replaced: a toast says so and Learn shows what replacing it would do now. While AI is editing, the style cannot be replaced and the button says why. In both tabs the dialog can close; the learning goes on in the top bar, which opens it again, and a toast reports the result. Stop ends it within a second.
- **Edit as text** shows the whole `EDIT.md` to read and change; `Ctrl+S` or Save keeps it as written, Cancel (or `Esc` while unchanged) leaves it. A rule changed or deleted there is left alone by learning from then on. If the style changed in another window meanwhile, saving says so and keeps the text.
- **Live**: a change made anywhere else, by an agent in a terminal, `nuzky style learn`, another window or a text editor, shows on the page within half a second, the changed rules lit up for a moment.
- **Learning by itself**: after every export (from the app or by an agent), and 10 s after you change a project an AI cut and its run was kept (or as soon as the project is switched or closed), Nuzky reads the project and the suggestions update: what you kept, cut and shortened, your captions and zoom, and the words you corrected, which become Spelling. It never learns from the AI's cut as the AI left it, nor from edits that undo the AI's run, and it changes nothing in the style until you accept. When that brings new suggestions, a toast says “Learned from <project>: 2 new suggestions” with Review.
- **Versions** (the clock): every change of the style, newest first: accepting, rejecting, removing, reverting, your rules, editing by hand, a change made to the file outside Nuzky, learning with `nuzky style learn` or from projects of another editor, and restores. Choosing one restores it as a new version, with Undo in a toast. Back to default… asks first, then removes the style, so the AI follows Nuzky's defaults, and forgets what was learned from your videos; a toast offers Undo, and Versions bring the style back.

### Launcher
Projects in the editor's top bar, or `Ctrl+K`, opens the launcher over the editor: a search field, Continue (the open project, with the AI run when there is one), the five most recent other projects, and All projects, which goes to the home screen. Below, New starts a project in a format or from videos, and Open file… opens one from anywhere. It searches like the home screen: projects by name, then Said in your videos, six of each; when there are more, Show all results continues the search on the home screen. The keyboard stays in the search field: `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` opens (the first result once you type), `Esc` closes and keeps editing; `Tab` reaches the buttons. While an agent edits, the question about its changes shows inside the launcher; `Esc` there cancels it.

### Errors
An error says in plain words what happened and what to do next; an error toast stays until it is dismissed. The engine's coded errors (`READ_ONLY: …`, `EDIT_REJECTED: …` and the like) are translated where they are shown; an error without a known code is shown as it came.

### Saving
Every edit is saved about a second later. The top bar shows Saving…, Saved, or a failure with an icon and text. An action that changes nothing, such as a failed import, leaves the status as it was.

## Keyboard

| Key | Action |
|---|---|
| `Space` | Play / pause |
| `S` | Split the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip |
| `Q` / `W` | Delete the part of the clip left / right of the playhead |
| `Ctrl+D` | Duplicate selection |
| `Delete`, `Backspace` | Delete selection or the selected transition |
| `Ctrl+Z` / `Ctrl+Shift+Z`, `Ctrl+Y` | Undo / redo |
| `←` `→` (`Shift`) | Step a frame (a second); on a focused clip, go to and select the previous or next clip (`Ctrl`: focus only) |
| `↑` `↓` | On a focused clip, go to and select the clip on the track above or below (`Ctrl`: focus only) |
| `Enter` (`Shift`) | On a focused clip, select it (add or remove it) |
| Menu key, `Shift+F10` | On a focused clip, open its context menu |
| `Alt+←` `Alt+→` (`Shift`) | Nudge the focused clip a frame (a second); on the main track, move it one place |
| `Home` / `End` | Start / end |
| `Ctrl` + wheel, `+` / `-` | Zoom timeline; the playhead stays where it is on screen (moved to the middle if it was out of view), with the wheel the moment under the pointer does |
| `F2` | In the transcript, correct the selected word |
| `Ctrl+I` | Import |
| `Ctrl+E` | Export |
| `Ctrl+J` | In the editor, open or close the AI panel; on the home screen, open the editor with it |
| `Ctrl+K` | In the editor, open or close the launcher; on the home screen, search |
| `Ctrl+O`, `Ctrl+N` | On the home screen, open a project file; open the New project menu |
| `F8` | Focus the newest toast; `Esc` there dismisses it and focus moves to the next toast, then back |
| In the cover editor | `←` `→` (`Shift`), `Home`, `End` choose the frame; `Delete` removes the selected text; `Esc` clears the selection, then closes the editor; `Space`, `S`, `Q`, `W` and `Ctrl+D` do nothing |
| `Esc` | Cancel a drag or close the open menu or dialog; with none of those, clear the selection |

Editor shortcuts are ignored on the home screen, in the launcher, while typing in a text field and while the export dialog is open. A focused tab bar keeps `←`/`→` (and `Home`/`End`) to switch tabs, so the playhead does not move. The focused transcript keeps `←`/`→`, `Home`/`End`, `Delete`, `Backspace` and `F2` for its words; while a word is being corrected, its field keeps every key.

## Interrupted AI edits

Opening a project with an unfinished AI checkpoint blocks the editor with “An AI edit didn't finish”. “Keep changes” keeps the saved edits. “Restore previous version” restores the checkpoint as one undoable step and offers Undo in a toast. The dialog cannot be dismissed before choosing; a failed recovery keeps it open with the error. While an AI run is open, the top bar shows “AI is editing · <label>” with Stop and edit, and the editing controls are disabled: the inspector, the tools in the left panel, the timeline's edit buttons, track toggles and new transitions, moving and trimming clips, the preview box handles, Ratio and renaming; in the cover editor Pick for me, the frames, the cover's settings and its handles, and choosing a frame on the timeline, which goes back to the agent's frame. Disabled buttons stay reachable by keyboard and give that reason; fields are disabled with it as their tooltip. Selecting, playing and scrubbing still work. An edit tried anyway, by shortcut or drop, is not sent and shows “AI is editing. Stop it to edit yourself.” with Stop and edit. That toast stays until the run ends; dismissed, it comes back with the next refused edit. When a run that changed something ends, “AI edit done” offers Undo for the whole run; for a run of the AI panel's agent, the panel's run card does that instead.
