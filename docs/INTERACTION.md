# Interaction spec

How the editor behaves. Visual tokens live in [DESIGN.md](../DESIGN.md).

## Layout

Nuzky starts on the home screen with every project; the editor of the last project is open behind it (see Home screen). The editor: top bar (Projects, name and save state in the middle, AI run, background jobs, AI, versions, Export) · left panel (Media, Audio, Text, Captions, Transcript, Transitions, Filters) · preview with transport and Ratio · inspector · timeline across the bottom, its toolbar starting with Undo and Redo, its main track starting with the Cover tile · the AI panel, when open, as a column at the right edge by default. The layout follows CapCut so its users find everything where they expect it. Each control exists once: the canvas format lives in Ratio under the preview, the timecode in the transport.

The library, the inspector and the timeline start close to CapCut's proportions (28 % of the width each, 35 % of the height) and follow the window until resized. Drag the gap right of the library, left of the inspector or above the timeline to resize that pane (a grip shows while the pointer is over it); double-click resets it to its share of the window, and ←/→ (↑/↓ above the timeline) on the focused gap resize by 24 px. The preview always keeps at least 340 px of width and 300 px of height: a pane stops there, and in a smaller window the inspector gives way first, then the library; both get their size back when the window grows. The sizes are remembered.

## Response time

| Action | Feedback |
|---|---|
| Click, toggle, select | Visual state change in the same frame |
| Seek or scrub | Last frame stays on screen until the new one arrives, so the preview never flashes |
| Import | Item appears at once with a skeleton thumbnail; audio preparation shows a thin progress bar on the item |
| Import of video that decodes slowly (HEVC from phones, AV1, VP9, larger than 1080p) | A lighter copy for the preview is made in the background, one file at a time: "Preparing preview of IMG_0042.MOV 42%" with Stop in the top bar. Until it is ready the preview plays the original; from the next pause on it plays the copy, which has the same frames at the same times. Thumbnails made after that come from it too. Export always reads the original. If making the copy on the GPU fails partway, the copy is made again in software and its percentage starts again from 0. A copy made by an earlier version of Nuzky is made again once. A failed copy says so in a toast and the preview keeps the original |
| Export, captions, transcript | Background job with percentage and phase in the top bar; the user keeps editing; a toast reports the result |
| Long work an AI agent started (transcription, export, finding the person, scanning) | The same top bar capsule: what it does and its percentage, measured as it goes (a transcription counts the speech recognised, then the words timed), with Stop. Stop cancels only that work within seconds; the agent sees it stopped and says so |
| Sound library search, preview, add | Searching shows skeleton rows at once; a sound that has to download shows a spinner on its picture (preview) or the percentage on its + (add), and the panel stays usable |

## Flows

### Import
Entry: Import button, `Ctrl+I`, or files dropped on the window. Files dropped on the timeline are also placed on it; while media files are dragged over the timeline, an `accent` line shows where they would land, or the clip they would replace is ringed (see Replace media). Each picked or dropped file shows at once in Media (and Audio for sound files) with its name and a skeleton thumbnail while it is read. The Audio tab's "Add music or sound", under In project, opens the picker filtered to audio.
Outcome: items appear in Media (all kinds) and, for audio, in Audio. Unsupported files produce one toast naming each file by name only with a short reason ("build.log is not a video, audio or image file"); the rest still import.
Hovering or focusing a media or audio item shows `+` (add at playhead), on videos and images a picture-in-picture button (see Add to timeline), and a trash button (remove the item and its clips at once; a toast offers Undo, like deleting clips).

**Video from YouTube**: From YouTube beside Import media (an icon only when the panel is narrow) opens a dialog for a link to one YouTube video, while the editor pauses. Pasting into the empty field or over all its text, or Enter, looks the video up with the same yt-dlp as music (see Sound library), giving it only the watch address built from the video's id; a skeleton line shows meanwhile. The first YouTube link ever, video or song, first shows in the dialog that the user is responsible for the rights to it and for following YouTube's terms, with Cancel and I understand; agreeing is remembered for music too. Then the title, the channel and the length show and the whole video downloads at once (at most 1080p, picture and sound apart, as no ffmpeg program is needed), with the percentage and Stop in the preview's place; stopped, Download again. Once downloaded and its sound prepared, the video plays in the dialog with sound, in a preview of its own that leaves the project's preview and playhead alone. A link with a time, as YouTube's Share writes it (t=83 or t=1m23s), opens the video there. The part to import is chosen in a filmstrip of the whole video under it, however long: dragging the strip scrubs, its two handles set the start and the end and show the frame there, and the Start and End fields take typed times (6:21.4, 381.4; ↑/↓ a tenth, Shift a second). A handle moves as far as the pointer does, wherever it was caught; held still for a moment while pressed, the strip of a longer video zooms in to about a frame a pixel around it until it is let go. A dragged handle keeps the focus, shown by its outline and the key hint Move start or Move end, so `←`/`→` then move it a frame (`Shift`: a second); elsewhere, and once playing starts, they step the playhead. `I` and `O` set the start and the end at the playhead, `Home`/`End` go to the ends, `Space` plays and `Enter` imports. These keys work as soon as the video shows, after a click on Play, and again right after Enter or `Esc` in a time field. The handles cannot cross and the part is at least a frame, so no choice is ever an error; `I`, `O` or a typed time past the other edge starts a new part there, running to the video's end or from its start, as in Final Cut. Playing from inside the part stops at its end, so the cut is heard as it will be; `Space` there plays it again from its start. The whole video is chosen at first, and Use whole video brings it back. The import button names the result: Import whole video, or Import 4.0 s. Import closes the dialog at once and the video shows in Media with a skeleton under its title, with Stop, while Nuzky joins picture and sound into one file in its own folder, without re-encoding, keeping the part and 3 s on each side; before the part it starts at the keyframe at or before those 3 s, so there can be a little more. The asset looks like the part: its tile, thumbnail and new clips show only the part, and dragging a clip's edge outward reveals the hidden footage, up to the file's real start and end; near the video's ends there is less of it, and none for the whole video. A failure says why in a toast with Open again, which opens the link again without downloading it anew. The downloaded video stays in the cache only until the next YouTube video downloads or Nuzky restarts. `Esc` or Close closes the dialog at any step, stops a download and adds nothing. A press beside the dialog closes it only before a video starts downloading, so a missed handle never throws one away. Errors (not one video, a playlist, private or removed, live, too long, offline, YouTube refusing) are a 12 px line under the field, with Try again where it can help; a missing or outdated yt-dlp offers Download yt-dlp there as in Music. While the AI is editing, From YouTube and Import are disabled with the reason. Agents add a video, or a part by start and end, with `add_youtube_video`.

### Add to timeline
`+` on a media item adds it at the playhead (video and images on the main track, audio on an audio track). Dragging an item onto a track places it at the pointer. The new clip is selected.

**Replace media**: dropping an item on the middle of a clip puts the item in that clip, the way a copy of a styled clip becomes the next shot. While it is over the middle, the clip shows a 2 px `accent` ring over an `accent` 15 % fill and a Replace chip instead of the drop line, or a `danger` ring and fill and a Too short chip when the item is shorter than the clip needs; within 24 px of each end (a quarter of a narrow clip) the item goes in beside the clip as before. The clip keeps its place, length, track, speed, size and position, keyframes, animations, transition, colour, shape, background and sound settings, and plays the new media from its start, or from the part chosen when it was imported. A video track takes a video or an image, an audio track a file with sound; elsewhere nothing is offered. Clean voice, Reduce echo, Match loudness and Even out volume turn off when the new media has no sound. Media too short for the clip at its speed changes nothing, and a toast gives both lengths, the speed when it is not 1x, and says to pick longer media or shorten the clip first. While Undo or Redo is hovered nothing is replaced. One file dropped from the desktop does the same after it is imported. The context menu's Replace media… picks the file with the system picker. One undo step brings the old media back.

**Picture in picture**: the button on a video or image item puts it at the playhead in a small window over the main track, for a screenshot or a second camera beside a talking head. The window's longer side is 45 % of the canvas's shorter side; it sits in the top right corner, against the corner of the Reels and TikTok safe area on vertical videos and 4 % of the shorter side from the edges on the others, with corners rounded at 15 % and a soft shadow. It goes on the first overlay track free there, else on a new one above the others. A video plays its whole length, an image lasts 3 s. One undo step; the new clip is selected, so the inspector shows its crop and shape.

### Sound library
The Audio tab has three tabs: In project (the project's own sound files, with Add music or sound), Music and Sound effects. It opens on In project and remembers the last one. Their searches show only sounds under CC0 or CC BY, the licences that allow monetized videos; anything else a catalogue returns never shows. The one exception is a song the user links from YouTube themselves (below).

Sound effects lists the effects built into Nuzky (CC0, they work offline) with chips for their kinds (Whoosh, Transition, Pop and click, Bell and alert, Impact, Funny, Everyday) that scroll with the list. Typing filters them at once and, after a short pause or Enter, also searches online; online results follow under Online. Music lists the songs built into Nuzky under Built in (CC0, they play and add offline, like the built-in effects; the first is "Dancing" by HoliznaCC0), then offers ideas (Upbeat, Chill…) and searches online only when the user types or picks one; nothing is sent before. Typing filters the built-in songs by title and artist at once. Online search uses Openverse (its Freesound and Wikimedia Commons sounds; music is searched by its words, as almost no other music there is filed as music; Jamendo's tracks stay out while its commercial terms are unsettled), or for sound effects Freesound when the user saved their own API key under Where to search (the sliders button beside the field; `Esc` closes it and returns there) and turned it on; the switch stays disabled until a key is saved. Only the typed text is sent. The results end with "Search uses Openverse. Nuzky is not endorsed or certified by Openverse." and the advice to check a sound's page, since catalogue licences can be wrong; More results loads the next page.

A row shows the title, the author and where it comes from (or the kind for a built-in one), the licence (CC BY or CC0) and the length. Clicking a row (or its picture, the keyboard's way) plays the sound through the app's own audio, downloading it first when needed (a spinner meanwhile); the row stays highlighted while it plays. Clicking again, another sound, Play on the timeline or leaving the tab stops it, and the timeline pauses while it plays. The licence opens the sound's page at its source in the browser. `+` on hover or focus, or a double click, adds the sound at the playhead on an audio track as one undo step, downloading it first (the `+` shows the percentage; clicking it stops the download, a double click does not). Library sounds are added with `+` or a double click, not by dragging. The file goes to Nuzky's sound library beside the projects, and the asset remembers the source, author, licence and page. A sound the project already has shows a check and adds another clip of the same file. While the AI is editing, `+` is disabled with the reason; playing and searching still work.

When a CC BY sound or video plays on the timeline, the bottom of the Audio tab says what needs credit ("1 video and 2 sounds need credit"), with Copy credits: it copies, for the video's description, each CC BY sound's title, author, page, licence with its link and what the video changed ("Synced to video; trimmed, faded."), then the CC0 ones as a courtesy, and a toast says where to paste it. The project's own CC BY audio shows a CC BY chip. Export writes the same text beside the video (see Export).

Offline, or when Openverse limits the searches, the online part says so in place of results with Try again (one line under the built-in songs and effects, which still show). A failed download says why in a toast and adds nothing.

Under the search field, Music has a field to paste a link to one YouTube or YouTube Music video (youtube.com, music.youtube.com, youtu.be, Shorts). Pasting into the empty field, or Enter, looks the video up; `Esc` clears it. Nuzky does not include a downloader: it uses yt-dlp, its own copy when it has one, otherwise the one installed on the computer, found on the `PATH` or where Homebrew, pipx, winget and Scoop put it. It gives yt-dlp only a watch address built from the video's id, never the pasted text. Without yt-dlp the line under the field says that music from YouTube comes through it and offers Download yt-dlp: Nuzky downloads the build for this computer from yt-dlp's newest release on GitHub, about 20 to 40 MB, with the percentage and Stop, checks it against the size and SHA-256 GitHub gives for it, keeps it beside the sound library and looks the link up again by itself. When the yt-dlp installed on the computer fails to look up or download a video for a reason Nuzky can't name, as when YouTube refuses an outdated one, the line says it is probably out of date and offers Download yt-dlp the same way; Play or `+` on the row then shows that line in place of the row, without an error toast; once this download finishes, Nuzky plays or adds the song as asked, and the downloaded copy is used from then on. A download from Get set up only looks such a link up again; it plays and adds nothing, and the song waits for its own Play or `+`. A stopped or broken-off download continues where it stopped the next time. Clearing or replacing the link stops it. Once a week, after yt-dlp has run, Nuzky looks in the background for a newer release and downloads it when there is one, as YouTube often breaks an old one; meanwhile and offline it uses the copy it has. A look that failed is repeated after ten minutes of use. The very first time, before anything is looked up, a dialog says that Nuzky downloads only the sound, that the user is responsible for the rights to the music and for following YouTube's terms, and that copyrighted music can get a video muted, blocked or demonetized; Cancel looks nothing up, I understand looks the link up and is remembered, so the dialog never comes again. While looking up, a skeleton row; then the video is a row like any other, with its title, the artist or channel · YouTube and its length, and no licence chip. Play downloads only its sound (AAC, as YouTube serves it, and never with a part missing) and plays it; `+` adds it at the playhead as music, with the percentage while it downloads and the same stop, and clearing or replacing the link stops it too. Once the project has it, the row shows the check and `+` adds another clip of the same file. The file goes to the sound library like the other sounds, and the asset has no credit, so Copy credits and the credits file leave it out. Every song added from a link stays in Music in every project, under From YouTube with its count, newest first, below the ideas: the same rows, which play and add from the file already on the computer, so they work offline and without yt-dlp. Typing in Music's search filters them by title and artist at once, and the online results follow, under Online when any of them matches; the linked song is not listed twice. Songs added before this list existed join it once their link is added to a project that does not have them yet. Lower under speech, volume, fades and the export's loudness levelling work on it as on any music. A link that is not one video, a playlist, a private, removed or age-restricted video, a live stream, a video too long for a sound (over 60 MB of sound, about an hour), no internet, YouTube refusing too many requests and a download of yt-dlp that did not check out each give one line under the field saying what to do, with Try again where trying again can help. Where Nuzky has no yt-dlp build for the computer, the line says to install it from yt-dlp's page. Nothing is added then.

### Edit clips
- **Select**: click. `Shift`-click or `Cmd`-click (`Ctrl`-click outside macOS) adds a clip to the selection or takes it out. Drag from empty space in the lanes, below the last track too, to draw a box: it selects every clip it touches, on any track, as it grows, and near an edge of the lanes the timeline scrolls on; with `Shift` or `Cmd` (`Ctrl`) it adds them to the selection. `Esc` during the drag puts the selection back. A click on empty space clears the selection and moves the playhead there, on a frame or, while snapping is on, onto a clip edge within 8 px, as the ruler does; `Esc` also clears the selection.
- **Keyboard**: the clips are one tab stop (the clip focused last, else the first selected, else the first on the main track). On a focused clip `←`/`→` go to the previous or next clip on its track and `↑`/`↓` to the nearest clip on the track above or below, without moving the playhead, and select the clip they reach, so `Delete`, `S`, `Q`, `W` and `Ctrl+D` act on the clip with focus. `Ctrl` with an arrow moves focus without selecting, to build a selection with `Shift+Enter` (adds or removes the focused clip); `Enter` selects only it. `Alt+←`/`Alt+→` nudge it a frame (`Shift`: a second), stopping at its neighbours; on the main track they trade places with the clip beside it. On one of several selected clips they nudge every selected clip off the main track together, stopping where one of them would reach a clip that is not selected, and the selection stays. A burst of nudges is one undo step. `Alt+↑`/`Alt+↓` move a video or text clip a layer up or down, onto a new track past the next video or text track above or below its own, so it covers or goes under what is there; a main-track clip stays, nothing goes under the main track, and the clip keeps the focus. The Menu key or `Shift+F10` opens the clip's context menu; closing the menu returns focus to the clip. When the focused clip is deleted, focus and selection move to the clip that took its place on the track, else the one before it. `Space` works as everywhere.
- **Move**: drag the clip body. A ghost follows the pointer and snaps within 8 px to the playhead and clip edges; a vertical line shows the snap. Dropping on an occupied spot creates a new track right above that one, and dropping above the tracks a new track right above the clip's own. Over the line between two video or text tracks an `accent` bar spans the clip's time, and dropping there puts the clip on a new track between them, so a picture can go under another one; the main track stays the lowest. `Esc` cancels the drag, and so does the system taking the pointer or the window losing focus. With several clips selected, dragging one of them off the main track moves them all by the same time, each on its own track, as one undo step, and they stay selected; the earliest stops at the start, and the main track's clips stay, as they sit back to back. When one of them would land on another clip, the selected clips of its track go together onto a new track right above it, hidden or muted like their own. Such a drag stays on the tracks; to move a clip to another track, drag it alone. A selected main-track clip, grabbed, moves along the main track alone.
- **Main track**: magnetic. Clips sit back to back; moving reorders, deleting closes the gap. Deleting a main-track clip also cuts its time from the other tracks, as trimming does below: captions, titles and overlays over it go, and the ones after it move left with their picture; audio tracks set to keep in place stay. While a clip is dragged the track shows the result live: the neighbours move aside, a dashed slot and an `accent` bar mark where it goes in, and the gap it left closes. Trimming a main-track clip's end moves the clips after it with the edge. Trimming its start moves only that edge while it is dragged, as in CapCut, so the rest of the timeline stays still and the trimmed part stays outlined; on release the clip slides back to where it started and the clips after it follow, so no gap opens. The other tracks move with it, so captions, titles and overlays stay over the same picture: the time the edge gains opens on them too and the time it loses is cut from them, as with `Q` and `W`; audio tracks set to keep in place (see Transcript) are left alone. Media dragged over the main track marks the cut it would go in at. Over another track it lands where the pointer is, or, while snapping is on, on a clip edge or the paused playhead within 8 px, as a moved clip does; the line shows where. A clip that goes in between main-track clips, dropped there, added with + at the playhead or duplicated, pushes the clips after it along, and the other tracks open the same time there, as when pasting: captions, titles and overlays after it move with their picture, a caption across the cut splits between its words, and audio tracks set to keep in place stay. A clip dragged down from another track leaves the other tracks as they are, because what sits at its old time, such as its detached sound, can be its own.
- **Trim**: drag a clip edge. A tooltip shows the new duration. While the edge moves, the clip shows the frames and sound it keeps: dragging the start in cuts the footage from the start. The handle stops at the end of the source media, taking the clip's speed into account.
- **Fade**: a clip on an audio track has a dot in each top corner, shown while the pointer is over the clip or it is selected; while there is no fade it covers the top of the trim handle, so trimming takes the edge below it. Drag the left dot right to fade the sound in from the start, the right dot left to fade it out to the end, as in CapCut. The dot stays at the end of the fade, the clip shows the fade as it changes, and a tooltip its length ("Fade in 1.2 s"). Fades go in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, whichever is shorter, and each drag is one undo step. `Esc` cancels the drag and leaves the fade as it was. The inspector's Audio tab sets the same fades from the keyboard, and the fades of a video clip's own sound. While the AI is editing, the dots are hidden.
- **Split**: at the playhead. `S` and the Split button split the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it, and are disabled with the reason when there is none; the context menu splits the selected clips under the playhead.
- **Cuts sound clean**: where two pieces of sound meet that do not play on from each other, they crossfade over 20 ms around the cut (10 ms on each side, reaching past the clips only into quiet sound of the files), so no cut clicks or dips; where the sound past the cut is not quiet, such as the start of a deleted word, the two sides fade out and in over 5 ms instead, so nothing deleted is heard. Split halves that play on, and cuts with a transition, are left as they are.
- **Delete left / right**: `Q` and `W` or the buttons next to Split remove the part of the clip left or right of the playhead, CapCut's fast way to cut a talking head. They act on the selected clips under the playhead, else the main-track clip under it, when at least a frame of the clip is left on each side; at the edge of a clip they say to move the playhead a frame away from it. On the main track the time is cut from every track, so captions, titles and overlays stay in sync; audio tracks set to keep in place (see Transcript) are left alone. A clip on another track is trimmed instead. After `Q` the playhead sits on the cut. Each press is one undo step.
- **Duplicate**: `Ctrl+D`, the Duplicate button or the context menu. The copy goes right after the original and is selected.
- **Detach audio and Rotate 90°**: the buttons after Duplicate. Detach audio does what the context menu's item does, for every selected clip with sound. Rotate 90° turns the selected videos, images and texts a quarter turn clockwise, every keyframe by the same angle, as one undo step; past 180° the angle is written the short way round (90° more than 135° is -135°). Each button is disabled with the reason when the selection has nothing it can act on.
- **Copy, cut and paste**: `Ctrl+C` or the context menu copies the selected clips, also from several tracks, and a toast says how many; `Ctrl+X` cuts them, with Undo in the toast. `Ctrl+V` or Paste at playhead pastes them at the playhead keeping their offsets to each other. The clips of each track go back on it when they all fit there, else on a new track right above it, as when dragging; a track a cut left empty is gone, so its clips go on the first free track of their kind, else a new one. Clips from the main track go in back to back at its cut nearest the playhead and push the main-track clips after it along, and the other tracks open the same time there, so their clips stay over the same picture, as when trimming; every other pasted clip keeps its offset to the main-track clip it starts over, so a take keeps its sound and titles also when the pasted takes close a gap between them. The pasted clips are selected and the paste is one undo step. The clipboard is Nuzky's own, never the system's: it keeps the clips as they were copied, and opening another project empties it, as their media belongs to the project. With nothing copied, `Ctrl+V` says so in a toast and Paste at playhead is disabled with that reason. With text selected, such as an error message, `Ctrl+C` copies the text. Copying works while the AI is editing; cutting and pasting are refused like other edits.
- **Paste attributes**: `Ctrl+Alt+V` or the context menu gives the selected clips the look of the copied clip of their kind (with several copied, the earliest one): a text takes its style, font, transform, keyframes and animations and keeps its own words (a caption keeps its wrap width); a video or image takes its Adjust, Background, Crop and shape, Motion and animations and keeps its place and size, the motion zooming from its own framing over its own length; an audio clip takes its volume, fades, Voice and Lower under speech. What the copied clip does not have, such as a background or an animation, the others lose. Clips of another kind are left as they were. It is one undo step, and a toast says how many clips changed, how many of another kind were left, and offers Undo. With no clip of the copied kind selected, it is disabled with the reason, which the toast also gives for the keys.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace`, the toolbar button or the context menu; all of them show a toast with Undo. With a transition selected they remove the transition.
- **Context menu**: right-click a clip for Split, Duplicate, Detach audio, Replace media… (one media clip; see Replace media), Select all on track, Cut, Copy, Paste at playhead, Paste attributes and Delete. Right-clicking a clip outside the selection selects it first; every item then acts on the selection, and with several clips selected the labels say how many (“Delete 3 clips”). Select all on track uses the clicked clip's track. Unavailable items are disabled with the reason in a tooltip; ↑/↓ reach them too and show the reason. ↑/↓ move, Esc closes.
- **Undo/redo**: `Ctrl+Z`, `Ctrl+Shift+Z` or `Ctrl+Y`, or the first two buttons of the timeline's toolbar, as in CapCut; the cover editor and the reels list, which have no timeline toolbar, keep them in the top bar before Versions. A slider drag, a typing burst, a preview drag or a caption restyle is one step. Undo in a toast undoes the step the toast names; once another change follows it, the toast no longer offers Undo.
- **What undo will change**: resting the pointer on the toolbar's Undo or Redo for 0.3 s, or reaching it with the keyboard, draws the timeline as the step would leave it, without changing anything. The clips that change are marked with a word as well as an outline, and the rest dim: a clip that comes back sits dashed in `accent` where it was (Back), a clip that moves or gets another length is outlined (Moved, Trimmed), and a clip whose look or sound changes is outlined (Changed). When the step takes clips away and the other clips keep their lengths and places, apart from closing the gap, the timeline stays as it is and those clips are hatched (Removed). A tip above the button names the step (`Undo · Delete`, `Undo AI run · <run label>` with a sparkle) and says what changes in words: what comes back or goes, a trim's lengths, a single value such as `Opacity 40 % → 100 %` or else the inspector's names of what changes, the name, the format or media, the video's length before and after, and how many changed clips lie off screen on which side. Past five changes it names four and says how many more. The timeline does not scroll and the preview keeps the current frame. Leaving the button or `Esc` puts the timeline back; an open menu or dialog takes `Esc` first. A click shows the result; the next step shows when the pointer or focus comes back. With nothing to undo the button says "Nothing to undo"; while the AI edits it says why it is off and shows nothing.
- **Versions**: the clock button in the top bar lists the newest 20 versions of the project, newest first: what made each (an AI run's label with a sparkle, or the step, such as Split, Undo or Restore) and when. A version is the project after each kept AI run, each of your steps, each undo, redo and restore, and as it was opened. They stay after a restart, up to the newest 200. The current one has a check. Choosing another restores it as one step, and a toast offers Undo; the versions after it stay in the list. `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` restores, `Esc` closes. While the AI is editing, the button is disabled with the reason.
- **Order**: edits, undo and redo apply one at a time in the order they were made. An edit made before the previous one is confirmed builds on it, so two quick changes (two sliders, two keyframes) both stick.

### Timeline display
Clips are drawn 1 px in from each side so every cut is visible. Video clips show a filmstrip whose frames match the source position under each tile, speed included (a single repeated thumbnail until the filmstrip is ready), and a thin waveform strip at the bottom while they have sound. A waveform draws a bar every 3 px of what is on screen, rising from the bottom edge as loud as the sound plays there at the clip's volume on a decibel scale, so a pause reads as a low line and turning the volume down lowers the bars; zoomed in further than the sound's detail, the bars blend from one peak to the next instead of stepping. While a file's sound is still being prepared after import, its waveform fills in from the start as the sound is decoded; parts not decoded yet stay empty. Clips not at 1x carry a speed badge ("2x"). Thin bars at the top edge show the length of the entry (left) and exit (right) animation. A sound fade on an audio clip shows as a line from the bottom corner up to where the fade ends, with the corner above it shaded. The selected clip shows its keyframes as diamonds; clicking one, or `Enter` on a focused one, moves the playhead there. Caption clips show only their text. When sound runs past the last picture or text, the video ends there: a dashed line on the ruler and lanes marks the end, the time after it is dimmed, and the ruler's tooltip says the sound after it is not exported.

### Transitions
A cut on the main track with a transition has a small square on the clips' top edge; clicking it selects the transition. An empty cut shows nothing until the pointer comes within 12 px of it or Tab reaches it: then a "+" shows in the same place, and clicking it adds a 0.5 s Dissolve and selects it. The square covers only the top corner of the clips' edges, so they trim below it. A selected transition opens in the inspector with its name, duration and Remove; the kind is picked in the Transitions tab. The Transitions tab applies to the selected cut, else the cut before the selected main-track clip, else the cut nearest the playhead, and names that cut. Duration is capped at 2 s and by the shorter neighbouring clip. None removes the transition.

### Filters
The Filters tab applies a preset (None, Vivid, Warm, Cool, Mono, Fade, Moody, Punch) to the selected video or image clip. A preset replaces the clip's Adjust values; the matching preset is marked. Without a media clip selected the tiles are disabled with the reason.

### Playback
`Space` plays and pauses; the focused control keeps focus and is not pressed. A focused checkbox or menu item keeps Space for itself. Clicking or dragging the ruler scrubs, and clicking empty space in the lanes moves the playhead too; the playhead lands on the nearest frame, or, while snapping is on, on the start or end of a clip on any track, captions included, within 8 px. `←`/`→` step to the next or previous frame, so a playhead between two frames lands on one; with `Shift` they move one second, onto a frame. Neither goes past the video's last frame. `↑`/`↓` jump to the previous or next start or end of a clip on any track, so the playhead can sit exactly where a caption starts. `Home`/`End` jump to the ends, also when the end falls between two frames. A focused slider keeps its own arrow, Home and End keys. The sound card drives the clock, so picture and sound stay in sync. Mute in the transport (`M`) silences the preview on this computer only: the picture plays on, the project and the export keep their sound, and the choice is remembered. Full screen (`F`) shows the preview with its transport over the whole screen, drawn in more pixels; `F`, `Esc` or the button leave it, and a window that was in full screen before stays in it. There the keys play, seek, mute and leave, while the keys that would change the hidden timeline (cut, split, delete, duplicate, paste, import, undo and redo) rest, and an AI run shows its bar with Stop above the video.

### Preview
Clicking the paused preview selects the top-most visible layer under the pointer, so a click outside the curve of a circle or a rounded corner reaches the layer below; clicking where no layer is clears the selection. The selected layer gets a box: drag inside it to move, drag a corner to scale uniformly, drag the round handle to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°). On videos and images, dragging the bar in the middle of an edge crops that edge: the rest of the picture stays where it is, a dashed outline shows the whole picture while dragging, and each edge stops 5 % short of the opposite one. An edge too short on screen for its bar to clear the corner handles has none; the inspector crops it. The box of a layer larger than the frame is cut off at the edge of the preview area, and handles that would leave it stay on that edge, so they can always be grabbed. Once the pointer has moved 6 px, the box snaps to the edges and middle lines of the frame when that moves it no more than 6 px on screen: moving snaps its edges and its centre, scaling and cropping the edges they move, and while dragging an orange guide shows on each line it sits on. A rotation snaps only to 15° steps with `Shift`. Each gesture is one undo step. If the clip has keyframes, the gesture writes the keyframe at the playhead. Ratio, right of the transport, switches the canvas between 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 and 4:5; in its menu ↑/↓ move through the formats and their Reframe buttons, and choosing or Esc returns focus to Ratio.

**Reframe**, beside each format in the Ratio menu (shown while its row is hovered or has keyboard focus), changes the canvas to that format and places the picture so the speaker's face stays in it: in the selected video and image clips, or with none selected in every one on the main track and any other that filled the frame (a picture in picture is left alone). It looks for the face twice a second in each clip as recorded, in the background: a chip in the middle of the preview's bottom edge shows “Following the face · 42%” with Stop, which stops it within a second and changes nothing, and the first time “Downloading the face model” (230 KB). The picture fills the frame upright with the face across the middle of what Reels and TikTok leave free (on other formats of the frame) and 40 % down it, as far as the picture reaches, so no edge shows. It holds still while the face moves less than 8 % of the frame across or 6 % down and then catches up on a smooth curve over 0.6 s, following a face that keeps going in one move, so the picture never floats; one wrong detection never moves it. Only a face the clip's crop leaves visible is followed; a clip with no face anywhere is centred. Each clip keeps its zoom beyond filling the frame (a punch-in stays), its crop and opacity; its keyframes are replaced by the ones written, which edit like any others, and a cut jumps to the next clip's framing. The whole change is one undo step: when done the chip says “Reframed 4 clips” (and how many had no face) with Undo and Done, any later edit hides it, and closing it by keyboard returns focus to Ratio. When nothing needed to change, a toast says so and nothing is added to the history. An edit made while the face is being followed fails the reframe with “The project changed while following the face. Reframe again.”, and a missing file of a clip it moves fails it with what to do. Reframe is disabled with its reason while the timeline has no video or image and while a reframe runs.

Shows the selected clip with CapCut's tabs, remembering the last tab per clip kind:

**Project status**: with nothing selected, the inspector lists the steps of a finished video, each read from the project as it is now and with one button that opens the tab doing it, with the keyboard on its control, so no control exists twice. Speech says Not transcribed or Partly transcribed (Transcribe: Transcribe timeline in Transcript), the retakes, filler words, hesitations and pauses Remove retakes and pauses would cut (Clean up: that button), or Nothing left to cut. Captions counts the captions and names their style when all of them have the same built-in one (Add: Generate captions; Edit: the Captions tab). Zooms offers Suggest (Suggest zooms in Transcript) once every heard clip is transcribed. Music names the audio file on an audio track, or counts them (Add: Music in Audio, in its search; Edit: In project in Audio). Until then Zooms says Needs a transcript. Cover says which formats have a cover (Make: the cover editor on Make it with AI; Edit: the cover editor). A finished step shows a green check. The steps only open a place, so they stay usable while the AI edits; the place itself is locked.

Adjust offers Exposure, Brightness, Contrast, Highlights, Shadows, Saturation, Temperature, Tint, Fade, and Vignette for single or multiple media clips. Each one scales or bends the picture, so black stays black (only Fade lifts it) and Brightness, Contrast and Shadows keep white white. Exposure, Temperature and Tint change the light as the camera recorded it, ±2 stops for Exposure; warming or cooling keeps the brightness, keeps skin close to its hue and only tints white faintly. Contrast is an S-curve around mid grey that never clips, gentler pulled down than pushed up. Shadows and Highlights expose the tones below and above mid grey by up to 1.25 stops; Highlights pulled down also dims the brightest tones, white included, by up to a fifth, so a bright sky gets its tone back. Brightness lifts or lowers the middle tones, less far down than up. A colour pushed past what the screen shows loses saturation instead of a single channel clipping, so skin keeps its hue.

While playing, values that follow keyframes update ten times a second; paused or scrubbing, they show the exact frame. Number fields keep what you type until Enter or blur, so "-" or "1," is never rejected mid-typing; Esc restores the value, ↑/↓ step (`Shift` ×10), commas work as decimal points. Changes preview live.

- **Keyframes**: the diamond in the Transform header adds a keyframe with the current values at the playhead, or removes the one there (filled diamond). Arrows jump to the previous and next keyframe. Once a clip has keyframes, every transform change updates or creates the keyframe at the playhead. A keyframe moves on to the next one at an even pace, or on a smooth curve when Motion wrote it; a new keyframe takes the curve of the one before it. Removing the last keyframe keeps its values. Reset clears the transform and all keyframes and keeps the crop at the playhead.
- **Crop and shape** (videos and images), right under Transform: Shape is Rectangle, Rounded (Corners from 1 to 99 % of half the shorter side, 15 % to start with, as a new picture in picture has) or Circle. Circle rounds fully and crops the longer side so a square is left, in the same undo step; the layer moves so the side nearer the canvas edge stays put, so a picture in picture in a corner stays in the corner and a centred layer stays centred. Crop left, right, top and bottom cut that share of the picture off, each stopping 5 % short of the opposite edge; the rest stays where it is. The crop is part of the transform: once the clip has keyframes, a crop change writes the keyframe at the playhead like the other transform values, so a crop can move, and a line says so. Border draws up to 40 px (canvas pixels) outside the edge in its color; Border color shows once it is wider than 0. Shadow puts a soft shadow under the layer, 0–100 %. Reset crop and shape shows the whole picture with plain edges again, every keyframe included. The preview and the export draw the same edge.
- **Background** (videos and images), below Crop and shape: None, Blur, Color or Image behind the person, like CapCut's background removal. Blur blurs the clip's own picture behind the person, Strength 5–100 % (50 % to start with), with the person left out of the blur, so the room fills in behind them instead of a halo of their colours; it is graded with the clip, Adjust included. Color (a dark grey to start with) and Image (an image of the project, covering the whole clip, so a crop or its move does not move it) show as picked, Adjust does not recolour them. Image takes the newest image of the project (never the clip's own picture), or asks for one to import when there is none; the image button, with a small picture of the image, lists the project's images and Import image…. Choosing the option already on keeps its strength, colour or image. The person is found once per file, in the frames the clips show, as a job, one file at a time: meanwhile "Finding the person · 42%" with a bar shows under the controls ("Waiting to find the person" while another file goes first), the top bar lists the job, and the preview shows those clips as recorded, switching part by part as the job gets through them, even while paused. A minute of 1080p video at 30 fps takes about 12 s, of 4K at 60 fps about 30 s. Export always has the background: it finds the person first when the job has not. Split, trimmed and copied clips of the file share what was found; another file, or the file changed, is looked at again. Choosing None, removing the clip or opening another project stops a job no clip needs any more; what it found stays. When the person model is missing a row says that showing and exporting the background needs it and offers to download it (1 MB); when the models cannot run on this computer the row says why and the options stay disabled. When the person cannot be found in a file, a toast and the row say why: the clip shows as recorded and None lets it export. The edge is a few pixels soft and follows the person from frame to frame without flickering; it suits one person filling much of the frame, as in a talking head. With several clips selected, a dash means they differ; a choice applies to all of them as one undo step.
- **Motion**: a slow camera move on a video or image clip in one click, below Background. Push in zooms in, Pull out starts zoomed in and ends on the clip's own framing, Ken Burns zooms in while drifting right; None removes the motion. A new move runs over the whole clip at the strength used last (10 % at first). Strength (6, 10 or 15 %) and Range rewrite the clip's move and are disabled with the reason until it has one. Range is the whole clip, or Rest of sentence: from the playhead to the end of the sentence said there, found in the transcript and kept inside the clip; without a transcript, or with the playhead off the clip, a toast says why and nothing changes. Picking Rest of sentence again moves the move to the sentence at the playhead. Another preset keeps the range of the move it replaces. The move starts and ends gently and is written as two ordinary keyframes, so Transform edits it like any keyframes, and each click is one undo step. On vertical videos it zooms about the middle of the Reels and TikTok safe area, otherwise about the centre of the frame, so a face framed there stays put and clear of the apps' buttons. The section marks the clip's move with its strength and range; keyframes the user changed by hand mark no preset. A preset on a clip with keyframes of its own replaces them, starting from the picture at the playhead, and None removes them keeping that picture; both show a toast that offers Undo. The tiles play their move while hovered or focused.
- **Several clips**: one change applies to every selected clip as one undo step, a slider drag included. Where the clips differ the field shows "—"; a typed value then applies to all. A transform change applies to the whole clip, every keyframe included, so "110 % on every clip" is Select all on track plus one value. Texts and captions share the whole Style section, so selecting every caption gives them one shadow: Bold, Background box and Shadow show a dash when only some have them, checking turns them on for all and keeps the colours and shadows of those that had them, and their own fields show once all have them.
- **Speed**: presets 0.5x–3x or a logarithmic slider from 0.1x to 10x. Speed changes the clip length; the tab shows the new duration and the source length used. Keep pitch, a checkbox shown when the clip has sound and is not at 1x, keeps a voice as high as at 1x instead of higher when faster and lower when slower. Moving a clip off 1x turns it on; turned off, it stays off until the clip is back at 1x. Clips sped up in a project from before it keep the changed pitch until it is checked. Playback and export sound the same. Captions, word times and cuts stay where speed puts them, the sound stays within 11 ms of there, and sound cut away does not come back. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut.
- **Animation**: In and Out each take one preset with a duration; the selected preset is marked and the slot label shows a dot when set.
- **Audio**: volume, fade in and fade out (in steps of 0.1 s up to half the clip or 10 s, like the dots on the timeline), Lower under speech. Detach audio moves the sound to its own audio track and silences the video clip; disabled with the reason when there is nothing to detach.
- **Voice**, a section under Audio: Clean voice, Reduce echo, Even out volume and Match loudness, in the order the sound goes through them, all off by default and all for speech. They change how the clip sounds, never when: cuts, crossfades, captions and word times stay on the same sample, the waveform and the transcript keep the original sound, and projects without them sound exactly as before. Each file's sound for them is prepared once in the background, a row with a bar under the setting saying what runs ("Cleaning voice · 42%", "Reducing echo · 10%", and "Measuring loudness" under Match loudness for both gains), the top bar listing the job; playback keeps what is ready and switches without stopping. When a preparation fails, its row says why with a triangle and the clip plays without that setting; export needs it, so it works once the setting is off. The file is not tried again until Nuzky restarts. Export always has everything; it waits for the preparation or does it itself. Turning a setting off, removing the clip or opening another project stops a preparation no clip needs any more. A split whose halves differ in a Voice setting crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have a setting; checking turns it on for all of them as one undo step, keeping the strength of those that have it, and the strength shows once all have it.
- **Clean voice**: a checkbox for speech recorded on a phone. A high-pass at 80 Hz takes away rumble and 50/60 Hz hum, gentle noise reduction (RNNoise with 15 % of the original mixed back, so speech does not sound processed) lowers steady room noise by about 15 dB between words, and a de-esser softens harsh s sounds by up to 6 dB. With Reduce echo, the echo is taken out first and the voice cleaned after.
- **Reduce echo**: a checkbox for speech recorded in a room that rings, with Reduce by (1–100 %, 80 % to start with) under it once checked. The sound without the room's reverb is made once per file on the computer, without a model or the network: weighted prediction error (WPE, as nara_wpe) takes away the reverb that can be predicted from the sound before it, then late reverb suppression turns down what the room's decay leaves 50 ms after the sound, by the reverb time measured from the file's own decays. At 100 % what rings on after a phrase drops by about 10 dB from a phone's one microphone and about 20 dB from two, at 80 % by about 7 and 13 dB; the voice loses only the reverb it carried, 1–3 dB of level, and steady room tone comes down a few dB. Reduce by mixes that sound with the original, so changing it is instant.
- **Even out volume**: a checkbox that brings the quiet and loud passages of a recording together, for a speaker who turns away or leans in, with Even out by (1–100 %, 50 % to start with) under it once checked. Every moment is compared with the speech within 1 s either side and moved towards the file's loudness by that share, at 100 % by up to 12 dB; the gain changes over about a second, so it never pumps on words, and pauses keep the gain of the speech next to them. Match loudness, when on, is measured after it. Peaks stay under −1 dBFS as with Match loudness.
- **Match loudness**: a checkbox that plays every recording equally loud, at −16 LUFS (EBU R128), so takes from different rooms and distances match. It measures the whole file as the clip's other Voice settings make it sound, leaving out pauses, so every piece of one take gets the same gain and keeps the take's own ups and downs. Volume still applies on top, and the end of the line shows the clip's resulting gain, Match loudness and Volume together, in dB (+6.2 dB). It moves a file by at most 20 dB and never pushes a peak over −1 dBFS: where it would, the gain dips 40 ms before the peak and comes back over 250 ms. Export presets then level the whole video to −14 LUFS with their limiter last, a step of about 2 dB.
- **Lower under speech** (ducking): a checkbox for music under a talking head, off by default. Checked, the clip goes 12 dB down while someone speaks and comes back in the pauses; Lower by, shown under it, sets 1–40 dB. Speech is the sound of video clips, also sound detached from them, on unmuted tracks: any 10 ms of their file louder than -40 dBFS, so other loud sound in a video counts too, while imported audio files never do and a clip that lowers itself never lowers another. The clip starts going down 150 ms before the first word, so the word is clear, stays down through gaps between words up to 250 ms and comes back over 400 ms, without clicks. Playback and export sound the same, Reels levelling to -14 LUFS included; projects without it sound exactly as before. A split whose halves differ in it crossfades like a cut. With several clips selected, a dash means only some have it; checking turns it on at 12 dB for the others and keeps the strength of those that have it, as one undo step, and Lower by shows once all have it.

### Text and captions
Text presets add a text clip at the playhead, centred in the frame, and select it; captions sit a little below the middle (y +0.15, where reels put them). Transform Reset puts each back there. The inspector offers the same style presets under the same names as the Text tab, or the Captions tab for a caption. The inspector calls a caption clip "Caption".

**Apply style to**: a caption's Text tab has Apply style to under the text field, All captions or This caption, as CapCut's Apply to all captions. Style (Font, Size, Color, Case, then the style tiles) follows right under it. On All captions, the styles and every style control below go to every caption of the caption's track as one undo step and show a dash where the captions differ; on This caption they go to the selected caption alone. The text field always edits the selected caption, and the Animation and Transform tabs and dragging in the preview stay the caption's own; a caption's Transform tab says so and points to Position in the Captions tab. Apply style to starts on All captions when Nuzky starts and keeps what was picked until it quits. It only chooses what the controls edit, so it stays usable while the AI edits.

**Font**: the Font field opens a searchable list. Fonts that ship with Nuzky come first, each name drawn in its own face; fonts installed on the computer follow. ↑/↓ move, Enter picks, Esc closes. Text without a font uses Inter, also on machines without it. A font the computer does not have shows a warning; the engine draws Inter instead. Presets carry no font, so the font survives a preset change.

**Captions**: choose language, accuracy, words per caption (1, 2, 3 or Phrases; 1–3 words also stay within 15 characters), style, then Font, Size, Color and Case, Highlight spoken word and key words, with More style folded under them as in the inspector, then Generate. Reel is the default style: Inter 95 px regular, white with a 7.5 px black outline, no box, 2 words. On vertical videos captions keep to the Instagram Reels and TikTok safe area (clear of the top bar, the like and comment rail and the caption and buttons at the bottom; 1080×1920: 250 px top, 180 px right, 500 px bottom, 60 px left): longer captions wrap instead of reaching under the app's interface, and restyling keeps that width. Captions keep a number with the word before it ("iPhone 17") and a sentence does not end on a lone word when the caption before can give one. Words per caption counts the words on screen, so a corrected word with a space ("v pohodě.") counts as two. A correction with more words than a caption holds ("Vzal bys ji?" at 2 words) is shown over consecutive captions, which share its time by their length, and each part lights up in karaoke styles over its share. A word too short to give each part at least a frame stays whole in one caption, since captions starting less than a frame apart would merge anyway. A caption never runs across a cut. Cutting speech after the captions were made takes the cut words out of them: a caption with words left on both sides of a cut becomes two, one with no word left goes, and a caption whose text was typed over keeps its longer side whole. The first run downloads the model with progress, unless Captions model in Get set up downloaded it already. Recognition runs in the background and can be cancelled; while it runs the fields are locked and a starting job shows an indeterminate bar. Captions and Transcript share the language and accuracy and one recognition at a time. The language starts as the computer's language when it is offered (else Detect automatically); the language and the accuracy are remembered once picked, here or in Get set up. Generating again replaces the previous Captions track, keeps the look of the captions there (style, font, animations and key words) and where they sit (the place most captions without keyframes share, so one caption moved alone does not move them all, at the opacity most of the shown ones share, so a hidden caption never hides them), and the button label says so. With captions on the timeline, every style control in the tab changes every caption at once, as one undo step, and shows a dash where the captions differ; words per caption apply when you regenerate. Position, a fold of its own under More style once there are captions, moves, scales, turns and fades every caption at once (Scale, Position X, Position Y, Rotation, Opacity, and Reset back to where captions start); a caption with keyframes keeps its own motion and a line under Position counts them. A caption's inspector has the same fold, Position of all captions, while Apply style to is All captions. An agent changes the look or the place of every caption the same way, in place (`restyleTrack`, or `build_captions` `transform` for captions it makes): each caption keeps its text and words, so one typed over by hand stays as written. A caption with keyframes keeps its motion, as its keyframes decide where it is; only its look changes. Before there are captions, the choices wait for Generate.

**Caption styles**: a style is a whole look for reels: size, colours, outline or box, the entry and exit animation of every caption, a karaoke highlight and key words, and for some a font from the bundled fonts. Picking one applies all of it to every caption; a style without its own font or case keeps the font and case the captions have, so a font or All caps picked separately survives it. The style matching every caption is checked, whatever font and, for a style without its own, case they have. A caption's inspector offers the same styles, for every caption or that caption alone as Apply to says, animations included. Agents pick the same styles by name (`build_captions` `style_preset`).

**Style and More style**: the Style section shows what a text needs most: Font, Size, Color and Case. The rest of CapCut's text settings wait folded under More style, so a beginner sees only those: Bold, Letter spacing, Line spacing, Alignment, Outline, Background box and Shadow. More style stays open or folded wherever it shows, for every text, caption and cover text, until Nuzky quits; it starts folded. Case is As typed (a circle with a slash), All caps (AB), All small letters (ab) or Each word capitalised (Ab); it changes only how the text is drawn, so the text field, the words of a caption, its karaoke highlight and its key words keep what was typed. Alignment lines the lines of a text up at the left, the centre (as before) or the right; the text stays where it is, and a text of one line looks the same either way. Background box puts a box behind the text, black at 70 % to start with; under it Box color, then Box opacity (0–100 %) and Corners (0–100 %: 25 % is the usual slight rounding, 100 % rounds a box of one line fully, as a share of the font size, so the corners grow with Size). Letter spacing (−50 to 200 %) and Line spacing (−60 to 300 %) add space as a share of the font size: letter spacing after every letter but the last of a line, line spacing only between lines, so a one-line text keeps its height. Spaced letters do not join into ligatures. Shadow, off by default, draws a soft copy of the letters and their outline under them, over the box: turned on it starts as CapCut's own shadow, black at 90 %, Blur 15 %, Distance 5 and −45° (down and to the right; angles run counterclockwise from the right as in CapCut), with its Color, then Opacity and Blur, then Distance and Angle under it. Blur (0 to 100 %) and Distance (0 to 100) are CapCut's numbers and follow the font size, so the shadow grows and shrinks with Size: Blur 100 % softens the edge over half the font size and Distance 100 puts the shadow a whole font size away. Angle runs from −180 to 180°; a typed 225 becomes −135, the same direction. The text stays where it was; the layer grows by the shadow's reach so nothing is cut off. A preset is a whole look, so applying one clears these settings, except the case when the preset has none of its own. Opening a CapCut draft brings its shadows, letter spacing and line spacing along with the numbers CapCut shows; a shadow with depth comes over flat and is listed among what did not come over.

**My styles**: + under Style saves the current look (the selected caption's, else the first caption's, else the look waiting for Generate) under a name typed in place; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels. A saved style is a tile like the built-in ones and applies the same way. Its … button on hover, a right click, or the context menu key on a focused tile offers Rename (also `F2`) and Delete; Delete shows a toast with Undo. Names are unique and never a built-in style's name; a refused name says why under the field, which stays open. Focus returns to the tile, or to + when there is none. Saving, renaming and deleting work while recognition runs, as they do not change the project; the AI lock stops them like every change. Generate captions stays in view under the choices. My styles are kept beside the projects, not in a project, so every project and an agent (`style_preset` by name) can use them, also after a restart.

**Key words**: Off, Emphasis, First, Last or Longest, with their colour. Emphasis colours the numbers and the words said louder than the rest, scored like the punch-in suggestions (`analyze(kind: "emphasis")`): how much louder than the median word, an exclamation, and a number; where a word sits in its sentence does not count, so some captions have none and key words lie at least 1.5 s apart. Words shorter than four letters are never key unless they are numbers. The words are scored once when the captions are made, from their sound (a file whose sound cannot be read counts numbers and exclamations only), so they stay key through a word correction, a split, a trim or a cut, and regenerating scores them again. First, Last and Longest mark that word of every caption instead. Like karaoke, a caption whose words were added or removed by hand colours none. The word being spoken takes the karaoke highlight over its key word colour, so the two combine. A caption's inspector offers Key words for that caption.

Saved style names are trimmed, contain 1–40 characters and are unique across saved and built-in packages ignoring case and treating underscores and hyphens as spaces. An unreadable or damaged styles file reports an error and is never overwritten when another style is saved.

**Karaoke**: the Karaoke style (Reel with the spoken word in yellow), Green box (bold white on a dark box, the spoken word green), Spotlight and White box light up the word being said. Each generated caption keeps when each of its words is heard; a word is drawn in the highlight colour from its start until its end, and nothing is lit between words, before the first or while the caption holds after the last. Only the colour changes, so the size and the line breaks stay put. Split, trim, `Q`/`W`, text cuts and Duplicate keep every word on its moment. Fixing words in a caption's text keeps the timing as long as it has as many words as before (a corrected typo lights up when the old word did); adding or removing a word turns the caption's highlight off, the inspector says so under Highlight colour, and regenerating brings it back. A caption's inspector offers Highlight spoken word with its colour.

### Stickers and emoji
The Text tab has three tabs: Text (the text presets), Stickers and Emoji. It opens on Text and remembers the last one. Stickers are Nuzky's own drawings: two arrows, a circle, an underline, a check, a cross, a heart, a star, sparkles, emphasis lines, a highlighter and a speech bubble. Emoji are Google's Noto emoji of Unicode 18 in their groups, with a button per group above the list that jumps to it and shows which group is in view. Typing in the search field finds emoji by name and by the words Unicode lists for them, so "fire" also finds the fire engine; Enter adds the first result, ↓ moves to the results and `Esc` clears the field. The swatch beside the field picks the skin tone of the emoji that have one, and Nuzky remembers it; emoji that mix two skin tones come only through `add_sticker`, from the emoji itself. In the grids the arrow keys move and Enter adds; the group buttons are one Tab stop, and ←/→ jump from group to group.

Clicking a sticker or an emoji adds it at the playhead as an image clip, centred, its longer side 30 % of the canvas's shorter side, for 3 s. It goes on the first overlay track free there, else on a new one above the others, and never moves the main track. One undo step; the new clip is selected, so the inspector shows its transform and animations, which work as on any image. The picture is drawn once at 1024 px into Nuzky's sticker library beside the projects and never changes afterwards, so a project keeps the same picture after an update brings new art; adding the same one again adds another clip of the same file. Nothing is downloaded: the art is built into Nuzky. While the AI is editing, adding is disabled with the reason; browsing, searching and the skin tone still work. Agents add stickers and emoji with `add_sticker`, by the emoji itself, its English name or a sticker's name.

### Transcript
Text-based editing, as in CapCut. Transcribe timeline recognises each video file whose sound plays on the timeline (music and other audio files are not listened to), once and in the file's own time; a captions run does the same and reuses what is already recognised. The transcript belongs to the file, so it follows every edit (cuts, trims, `Q`/`W`, speed, detached sound, mute, undo and redo), is still there after a restart and is reused when the file is in another project. The refresh button recognises every file again. After recognising Czech or English, the job measures where each word starts and ends in the sound ("Measuring word times"), so cuts in speech without pauses land between words; the first time, it downloads that language's word timing model with progress ("Downloading word timing model"), unless Captions model in Get set up downloaded it already. Without the model, for example offline, the transcript still comes, with word times estimated as before, and the toast says the word times are estimated. Cancel stops it within a second. When a clip's file has no transcript yet, a note says "1 clip not transcribed" with Transcribe; until then Delete, Remove pauses and Remove retakes and pauses are disabled with that reason. The words flow as text in paragraphs that break where speech pauses for a second or more, each led by its timecode. Pauses longer than the threshold (0.5 s by default) show as chips with their length. A sound between two words that recognition did not write, such as an "eee", shows as a hesitation chip with its length ("Hesitation 0.3 s") when it lasts 0.15 s or more and quiet parts it from both words, whatever the threshold; sound that runs straight into a word is taken for that word. Hesitation chips appear once the file's sound is prepared, and not in recordings where speech is not clearly louder than the room, such as over music. While playing, the current word is highlighted and kept in view.

- **Select**: click a word to jump there and select it; drag or `Shift`-click to extend. With the text focused, `←`/`→` move word by word (`Shift` extends), `Home`/`End` jump to the ends, `Enter` jumps to the selection, `Esc` clears it, `Space` plays without leaving the text.
- **Delete**: `Delete`, `Backspace` or the Delete button in the bar under the text, which shows the selection's word count and length. It cuts the selected words and the silence around them, keeping 0.12 s after the word before and 0.08 s before the word after, counted from where those words are heard in the sound, so a cut never clips a syllable, out of every track except audio kept in place, so captions and overlays stay in sync, as one undo step. A selection of pause and hesitation chips alone removes them; a hesitation goes like a deleted word, with 0.12 s and 0.08 s of the silence around it kept as for words. Hesitations selected together with words go with them. The playhead lands on the cut; a toast with the time removed offers Undo. While the bar shows, toasts sit above it so they never cover Delete. Inside the text these keys never delete timeline clips.
- **Remove pauses**: the button names the count and the time it removes ("Remove 5 pauses · 6.7 s") and cuts every pause chip at once. A pause between words keeps half the threshold next to each word, so a 2 s pause at 0.5 s becomes 0.5 s (where the next clip starts speaking at once, the whole pause stays after the last word of the clip before, so no sliver of a clip is left); silence longer than the threshold before the first word and after the last one is trimmed to 0.08 s and 0.12 s. Pauses and text cuts only lie inside clips that carry speech: clips in which words were recognised. A clip without words, such as B-roll, is never part of a pause and never cut from the text.
- **Remove retakes and pauses**: the prominent button under the pause row cleans up a talking head in one step, as an agent's rough cut does. It cuts the earlier attempts of every restarted sentence, keeping the last complete one, attempts that trail off before a new sentence starts, filler words and every hesitation and pause chip, as one undo step. Hesitation sounds that recognition wrote ("ehm", "hmm") go anywhere; words such as "jakoby" or "prostě" go at the start of a sentence when a comma or a pause of 0.15 s follows them, and inside one when commas or pauses set them apart on both sides ("Mluvte, jakoby, ke kamarádovi", not "Vypadá to jakoby snadno"). Filler words are looked for in Czech and English only, where they cannot mean something else (German "Um zehn Uhr"); in other languages the button cuts retakes, hesitation chips and pauses. Two sentences that differ in a negation, a number or a content word are not taken for attempts at one, and both stay. A sentence that says again how the one before it ended after a comma stays too, and so does that ending: words alone cannot tell a retake from a repetition the speaker meant. Words go with the silence around them as with Delete, pauses shrink as with Remove pauses, and the captions follow. A toast with Undo names what went and the time removed ("Removed 3 retakes, 2 filler words, 1 hesitation and pauses over 0.5 s (12.6 s)"). The button is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed and when there is nothing to remove.
- **Correct a word**: double-click a word, or press `F2` with one word selected, and type what was said; `Enter` or clicking elsewhere saves, `Esc` cancels. The word then reads that way in the transcript, in the captions that show it (their style and timing stay) and in captions generated again, also after a restart. A word shown in parts over several captions stays one word in the transcript; correcting it again changes each of those captions: every part keeps as many words as it had and the last takes any extra, and a caption left without a word goes. That happens only while every part reads exactly as Nuzky made it: when one of them was deleted, moved off the word or typed over, none of them changes, so nothing the user wrote is overwritten; generating the captions again shows the corrected word. An agent gets those captions back in `captions_kept`. The correction belongs to the project and to that recognised word: if the file is recognised again and the word comes out differently, it no longer applies. Typing the recognised text again removes it. A corrected word is underlined with dots and its tooltip says what was recognised. A toast offers Undo; one undo takes back the word and its captions. If the word no longer reads what the transcript showed when typing began, nothing changes and a toast says the transcript changed meanwhile. While AI is editing, a word cannot be corrected and the notice says why. An agent's `correct_words` names the word it means the same way (`expected`); a word that reads otherwise refuses the whole call and nothing changes.
- **Suggest zooms**: the button next to the word count marks the sentences said with emphasis for a subtle punch-in: louder than the median sentence, an exclamation, or the opening hook; 1.2–8 s and three words or more, at least 5 s apart and about one per 12 s of timeline, each with its zoom (1.15–1.3×, more for a sentence that stands out more). A bar above the text says how many, with Dismiss and Apply N zooms. Apply takes focus when the suggestions arrive, `Esc` in the bar dismisses them, and focus then returns to Suggest zooms. Apply zooms in on every marked sentence as one undo step and a toast with the count offers Undo: main-track clips are split in the silence before and after the sentence, midway into it and at most 0.15 s from the words. When only silence lies between that point and a cut, or the cut is less than 0.3 s away, the zoom reaches the cut instead, so every piece of a take keeps words and its pauses can still be removed. The pieces in between scale up by the factor, keeping their position and rotation. The sound does not change. Clips with keyframes keep their own motion, and the toast says how many did. The suggestions go when the speech changes. Suggest zooms is disabled with the reason while clips are not transcribed or recognition runs, Apply while clips are not transcribed, and both, like every editing control, while the AI is editing.
- **Keep in place while cutting**: one checkbox per audio track, saved with the project and undoable. Tracks made for music and sound files start checked and keep playing across cuts; sound detached from a video starts unchecked, so it is cut with the picture. The setting also applies to `Q`/`W`.

### Export
`Ctrl+E` or Export opens a dialog: preset, resolution (720p, 1080p, 1440p, 4K, with the resulting pixel size for the canvas), frame rate (24–60, default the project's), quality (High, Recommended, Smaller file) with an estimated file size. Preset opens a menu: Custom, then the presets that fit the canvas, then the others, disabled, each with what it needs ("Needs 9:16") and the full reason in its tooltip. The presets are Reels & TikTok (9:16, 1080×1920, 30 fps), YouTube Shorts (9:16, 1080×1920), YouTube 1080p and YouTube 4K (16:9, 1920×1080 and 3840×2160), Instagram feed (4:5, 1080×1350, 30 fps) and Square (1:1, 1080×1080, 30 fps); fitting ones show their size and fixed frame rate in the menu. Picking one sets its resolution, its frame rate (the YouTube presets start from the project's, as YouTube wants the rate the video was shot at) and Recommended, and the dialog says "Loudness −14 LUFS, applied to the file": the export writes H.264 High with AAC 48 kHz stereo and levels the sound to −14 LUFS integrated with true peak at most −1 dBTP (ITU-R BS.1770), while the project and the preview keep their own levels. Quality can change within any preset, and the frame rate within the YouTube ones (up to 60); another resolution, or another frame rate where the preset fixes 30, switches back to Custom. When the canvas changes to a format the chosen preset does not fit, the preset says to switch Ratio to its format and Export… is disabled with that reason; no hidden crop or stretch. Preset, resolution and quality are remembered; a remembered preset comes back only on a project it fits. A preset export first measures the loudness (the dialog's progress says "Measuring loudness" for the first few percent), then renders; Cancel stops either phase within a second. Export… asks where to save and adds `.mp4` when the name has no extension. An existing file is only replaced once someone said so: the save dialog asks for the name it returns, and when the final name differs (`.mp4` was added) or the file appears while rendering, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?” with Choose another name… and Replace. Tab stays inside the dialog. It can be closed at any time, with `Esc` too unless an export is running (then Keep editing or Cancel export says what happens to it), and focus returns to what opened it; a running export shows its percentage in the top bar and clicking it reopens the dialog. Progress shows percentage and time left, with Cancel. When CC BY sounds from the sound library or a CC BY video, such as the sample, play in the video, export also writes their credits beside it as `<name>.credits.txt` (`reel.mp4` gets `reel.credits.txt`), and an existing credits file is replaced only once someone said so for that file: the dialog asks “reel.credits.txt already exists. Replace it?” on its own, since replacing the video is not consent to replace notes kept under that name. Success offers Show in folder, in the dialog or, when it is closed, in a toast. Failure shows the reason and Retry (same file); with the dialog closed a toast offers Details. Export is disabled with a reason while the timeline is empty.

Under the title, Video, Cover and Recap choose what to export; the dialog opens on Cover in the cover editor (`Ctrl+E` there too). Cover offers the format (9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube, with its size) and the file: PNG, or JPEG at the best quality under 2 MB, YouTube's limit. PNG is the default for the cover and JPEG for the YouTube thumbnail; the choice is remembered per format. Export… asks where to save in Pictures, named after the project ("Morning routine cover.png", "Morning routine YouTube thumbnail.jpg"), adds the extension when missing, and replaces an existing file only as the video does: the save dialog asks, and when the final name differs or the file appears meanwhile, the dialog asks “<name> already exists. Replace it?”. The export is a job (Rendering, Finding the person when the cover needs it) with Cancel; it writes through a temporary file, so a cancelled or failed export leaves nothing behind. A format without a cover says so and offers Make the cover, which opens the cover editor on it. Success offers Show in folder; with the dialog closed a toast reports the result.

Recap makes a short vertical video of how the video was cut, to show it off. A bar across it is the whole recording, with every cut in red. The recording races along it: kept footage plays fast, while each cut slows down to stay on screen 0.4 s, or as long as it lasts when shorter, and less when there are so many cuts that they would take more than half the race, the picture steps back, a red label under the bar says what goes (Retake, 4 words, Pause 1.2 s) and the transcript's cut words are struck through as they pass. When a cut has played, its piece of the bar snaps shut with a quick swish, so the bar and the length at its end shrink cut by cut. Cuts too close to tell apart on the bar go as one piece with one label, and when cuts follow each other quickly the picture stays back between them. Then, for 1.6 s, the new length lands big over the outline of the old one, with the old length struck through and what went. It ends with the first 4 s of the finished cut with its own sound, its captions and a thin bar of its length for 2 s, then a 1.5 s card “Cut in Nuzky · nuzky.app”, which End card turns off (remembered). The music of the finished cut, a sound on an audio track that ducks under speech or plays at least 10 s, plays from the start of the recap even when the video brings it in later, 9 dB lower while the recording races so the swishes stand out, carries on through the finished cut and fades out over the end card; the rest of the finished cut's sound plays only with its picture. Without music the race is silent but for a whoosh into it and the swishes. The race takes about a sixth of the recording's length, between 2.5 and 5 s, never plays slower than it was recorded and ends once the last cut has snapped shut, so a recap is at most 14 s; where kept footage plays above 8× it shows ten pictures a second. A recap is 1080 × 1920 at 30 fps, made on this computer from the project, its versions and its transcripts, and nothing is sent anywhere. Videos exported as Video or Cover never carry Nuzky's name.

Shows chooses what the recap covers. Everything cut since import compares the project with the recording as imported: every video the main track plays now or played in a kept version, whole, in the order they were imported. A video that moved from the main track to another track is footage over the recording, so it is not counted as cut. An AI run compares the project before and after that run, as its run card does; Shows lists the eight newest runs kept in Versions. Result gives the numbers the recap will show, the same as the run card's: the lengths, then the words, retakes and pauses removed, leaving out the ones that are none. A retake is an attempt at a sentence that was said again, removed while another attempt stayed, an attempt the speaker gave up on, or a whole take dropped from the main track; a pause is a cut without a word. When a file that was cut has no transcript, the recap shows no words, retakes or pauses, and the dialog names the file. While nothing was cut, Result says so and Export… is disabled with the reason; so it is when a run's versions are no longer kept. Saving, replacing, progress, Cancel and Show in folder work as for the video; the name starts as "<project> recap.mp4". Recaps run one at a time with exports.

### Covers
A 9:16 cover for Reels and TikTok (1080 × 1920) and a YouTube thumbnail (1280 × 720), one of each per project, made from a frame of the video with text that can go behind the person and images such as a logo over it. The Cover tile at the start of the main track, as in CapCut, opens the cover editor: it shows the 9:16 cover as it exports (else the YouTube one), or an empty shape until there is one, and is disabled with the reason while the timeline is empty. While the editor is open the top bar says so: Back to video in place of Projects, "Cover" before the project's name, and Export cover in place of Export. Back to video, clicking the tile again or `Esc` (the first `Esc` clears what is selected on the cover) closes the editor; the playhead and the clip selection come back as they were.

- **The editor**: the preview shows the cover in place of the video, drawn by the engine exactly as it exports, with nothing over it but the box of what is selected and the status chip, so it looks as it will on the profile or on YouTube, and the transport becomes the cover bar: the frame's time and 9:16 Cover or 16:9 YouTube. The library's place, titled Frames, holds Make it with AI until either format has a cover, Pick for me and the frames it found (the seven tabs act on the video, so they rest), the inspector the cover's settings, and the timeline chooses the frame: clips cannot be selected, moved or trimmed there, and nothing plays. Switching the format only changes what is shown.
- **The frame**: the playhead is the cover's frame. Clicking or dragging on the timeline, `←`/`→` (`Shift`: a second), `Home` and `End` choose it, one undo step per gesture or burst of keys; a frame changed by Undo, a candidate or an agent moves the playhead there. The frame time follows cuts as the engine keeps it on the same picture; a frame cut out of the video says so and asks for another.
- **A cover made from an image**: an agent can make a cover from an image of the project, such as one it generated, in place of the frame. The cover bar then shows Use frame, preceded by Image where the bar has room, in place of the frame's time; its tooltip names the image, and the playhead moves without changing the cover; Use frame, a found frame or Pick for me make the cover from a frame again, as one undo step, keeping its texts and settings; meanwhile Pick for me is secondary with the line "The cover is an image. Choosing a frame replaces it." and the hint above the timeline says that moving the playhead keeps the image. While the AI edits, Use frame is disabled and says why. Everything else works as on a frame: the person is cut out of the image for text behind them, an outline or a background, and the export draws it the same. Removing the image from the project takes the cover back to its frame.
- **Pick for me**: a job that looks through the whole video (Looking through the video, then Scoring frames) and cuts the person out of the best frame (Finding the person), with its phase, percentage and Stop. The first time it downloads the cover models (196 MB) in the same job, as the line under the buttons says; Stop works in every phase. It sets the cover to the best frame as one undo step, making the cover when there is none; a frame chosen while it ran is kept and a line says so. The frames it found (6 to 8, fewer in a short video, at least 2 s apart, best first) show as tiles with their time and why in plain words on one line ("Sharp, well framed, facing you"), the whole reason and their score out of 100 in the tooltip, and as dots on the ruler with the same tooltip. Frames that score under 40 % of the best, such as a blink beside open eyes, are left out. They serve both formats; Pick for me, then Pick again, scores the framing of the format shown. Before the first pick a line under it says what it finds; the timeline's own line says that clicking it chooses any frame. The grid is one Tab stop: the arrows move, `Enter` or `Space` chooses, which sets the frame and moves the playhead. Without a face in the video a line says the frames are the sharpest and best lit instead. Where faces cannot be found on this computer, Pick for me is disabled with the reason; choosing a frame and text in front still work.
- **Before there is a cover**: the editor shows the cover it would make at the playhead; nothing is saved until the first change (a frame, a text, a setting) or Export cover, which makes it as one undo step. A format without a cover, while the other has one, starts from that one: the editor opens on its frame and shows its outline, texts and images, the texts' sizes scaled to the format and each image in its top right corner, as large for that format's size. A YouTube thumbnail of a vertical video is laid out as agents lay it out: the frame 1.4 times larger on the left third with its top at the top, so the cut of the shoulders falls below the bottom edge, and the texts beside the person over the other two thirds, in front of it; with the cover models installed, the frame blurred and dimmed fills the rest and a white 10 px line goes round the person.
- **Text**: Add text puts "YOUR HOOK" in the Outlined look in front, in the lower middle of a cover, under the face of a talking head and inside the Reels safe area (beside the person on a YouTube thumbnail of a vertical video), selects it and selects its words in the field, so typing replaces them; Behind person is one switch under the looks. Texts are listed under Add text, with Behind person and a warning where the person covers too much of one; `Enter` on a row selects it. Click a text on the cover to select it, drag it to move, a corner to scale, the round handle to rotate (`Shift` snaps to 15°), snapping as in the preview; each gesture is one undo step. A selected text shows the text itself (a burst of typing is one step), the looks Big, Yellow box and Outlined (the ones agents use; a look sets the style only and keeps the text's font, like text presets), Behind person, then Font, Size and Color. More style (Bold, Outline, Background box) and Position (scale, position, rotation, opacity, Reset) are folded until opened. Delete and Duplicate are in its header; `Delete` removes it with Undo in a toast.
- **Images**: the cover is edited on its own, so a logo or another picture goes on it without going into the video. Add image, beside Add text, opens the file dialog for one image, imports it into the project like Import does and puts it on the cover; once the project has images, it opens a menu with Import image… first, then the images. Added from the menu, the image's Size takes the keyboard focus. The image keeps its transparency and lands in the top right corner, on a cover inside the Reels safe area, as large as a square a fifth of the cover's shorter side, so a wide logo stays readable, but at most two fifths of it long; it is selected and the inspector shows it. Images draw in front of the person and their line and behind the texts in front, and need no cut-out. They are listed under the texts by name; `Enter` on a row selects it. On the cover an image is selected, dragged, scaled and rotated like a text, each gesture one undo step; the inspector has its Size (100 % is as large as fits on the cover), Position, Rotation, Opacity and Reset, which puts it back in its corner, and Delete in its header. `Delete` removes it with Undo in a toast; it stays in the project. A cover takes up to 10 images; then Add image says so. Removing the image from the project also takes it off the covers, in the same undo step.
- **The person**: Behind person draws the text behind them, Line around person draws a line around them (colour, 1–40 px), and the Background is the frame As filmed, Blurred (Blur 1–100 %, Dim) or a Color. All but As filmed need the person cut out of the frame, which happens on its own as a job a moment after the frame stops changing; meanwhile the cover shows the whole frame with the text in front and a chip on the cover says "Finding the person" with the percentage and Stop. Without the cover models, Behind person and Line around person offer Download 196 MB in place of their switch, and a line under Background says that Blurred and Color download them; either turns the setting on and downloads the models, with the chip on the cover showing the download and Stop, and the cover shows the setting once the person is found. When a setting is on and the models are still missing, the chip offers Download. Where they cannot run, the settings are disabled and say so. When the person covers more than 30 % of a text behind them, a `warn` triangle marks the text in the list, and the inspector says how much and to move it up or make it smaller. When nothing stands out in a frame, the text stays readable in front of what is there.
- **The picture's own place**: clicking the picture selects it and opens the folded Picture in the inspector; it moves and scales like a text, and Picture sets its zoom, position and rotation, with Reset.
- **Removing**: Remove cover (or thumbnail) removes it with Undo in a toast.
- **Media missing**: the cover says which file is missing and to put it back, for an image on it also to take the image off the cover; Pick for me and the export say so too.
- **A damaged model**: when a cover model does not load, Pick for me says "A cover model is damaged. Download the cover models again." on every system, also where the models' folder has a space in its path, as `~/Library/Application Support` on macOS, never the model runtime's own error. Download again checks every cover model against its checksum and downloads the damaged ones again.
- **Make it with AI**: until either format has a cover, the prominent button in Frames, with a line saying it picks the frame and writes the hook from what is said in the video, or, when it cannot start, why; then a text button in the Frames header, as it makes both covers again. One click starts the agent on both covers in the AI panel, which opens (see AI panel). Without the cover models it downloads them first, the download showing in Frames with Stop and "Then the AI makes the cover", and starts once they are there, unless another project opened meanwhile; when the agent got busy meanwhile, a toast says why it did not start. It is disabled with the reason when the agent is not installed, is answering, another agent edits, or faces cannot be found on this computer.

### Reels from a long video
A long recording (a podcast, a talk, a stream) becomes several reels: the AI finds the moments that work on their own, the user picks and trims them, and each becomes a 9:16 project of its own. The long video itself never changes. It opens from Reels from a long video… in the home screen's New project menu, which asks for one video, makes a project of it and opens the mode with the keyboard on Find reels, and from Reels… in the Transcript tab of a project 3 minutes or longer (or one that already has reels). Done in the preview's bar or `Esc` goes back to editing, with the playhead and the clip selection as they were; opening another project leaves the mode.

- **The screen**: the panes keep their places, as in the cover editor. The reels take the timeline's place as cards, best first; the library's place shows the words of the reel chosen, to trim them; the inspector gives the preview its room, and the preview's bar plays the reel. The seven tabs and Export rest, as the mode changes no clips.
- **Find reels** opens the AI panel and sends it a prepared message: find the moments that work on their own and propose them for the user to pick, transcribing first when needed; the agent proposes them with `propose_reels`. A proposed reel whose first words are said in under 0.6 s of a shot the video itself cuts away from starts at the first word after that cut; trimming brings those words back. While its agent answers, the list says the AI is finding reels; the cards arrive live as it proposes them, then the list says Find again, which replaces the reels not made yet. Without Claude Code or Codex on the computer and without ChatGPT signed in with its plan allowed, the list asks to sign in, with ChatGPT account…, Check again and Connect an agent in a terminal…; such an agent finds reels when asked to. When the agent found none, the list says so and how to ask it; when it failed, the AI panel says why.
- **A card**: the title, the hook (the sentence the reel opens with), its length once cut, why it works and its score, and the reel's cover once something makes one, an empty vertical shape until then. A reel over 60 s shows its length with a warning; it can still be made. Clicking a card, or the arrows on the focused list (`Home`, `End`), shows its reel; `Space` plays it, `Enter` checks or unchecks it (restores a rejected one, opens a made one), `Delete` rejects or restores it, `Ctrl+A` checks every proposed one. Its checkbox and Reject or Restore do the same by pointer. A rejected reel stays in its place, dimmed. Each change is one undo step.
- **Playing**: choosing a reel moves the playhead to its start. Play runs it to its end and stops there; Play again starts it over. The bar shows the playhead, Reel start (`Home`) and Hear the ending (`End`), which plays its last 3 s; the reel's span in the recording stands over its words. The preview shows the recording itself, so pauses play at their full length; with Center crop, the parts of the picture the crop leaves out are dimmed.
- **Trimming**: the words of the reel are marked in the text, with up to a minute of what is said around them, never reaching into another reel not made yet. A click plays from a word; `Shift`-click moves the nearer end of the reel to it: before the reel or in its first half the start, else the end. In the focused text `←`/`→` go word by word, `Alt+←`/`Alt+→` move the start and `Shift+←`/`Shift+→` the end by a word, and the playhead goes to the new start or 3 s before the new end. The words change at once and the length once cut follows as soon as Nuzky has measured it; a burst of keys is one undo step. A reel can start and end on any word. A made reel keeps its words. When a cut made since moved the reel's words, the text says so instead, and its card says so in place of why it works: it cannot be checked or made, and Find again proposes it anew. While the AI edits, the line under the words says why trimming waits.
- **Making**: the 9:16 choice is Center crop (the middle of the picture fills the frame) or Blurred background (the whole picture over a blurred copy), remembered. ### Connect your agent
"Connect an agent outside Nuzky…" in the AI panel's agent menu opens a dialog with Agent access, Claude Code, Codex and Gemini, and copies for other agents.

- **Agent access**: a switch that decides whether agents outside Nuzky attached to the open project may edit it (those set up by Connect or a copy, which run `mcp --current`). ### Your style
Your style in the home screen's sidebar, under All projects, shows the style the AI follows when it edits: the file `EDIT.md`, which every agent connected to Nuzky reads as it is. Its count, in `accent`, is how many suggestions wait; without any it shows no count. Nothing of it leaves this computer.

- **Suggestions**: what learning found that is new or would change a rule of the style, each with what it says, the change (“Longest pause: 120 ms → 400 ms”), the confidence (High when it holds in 3 or more videos or at 10 or more moments, Medium in 2 videos or at 3 or more moments, Low otherwise, as its tooltip says, so one project worked on for long can be High) with in how many of the videos learned from and at how many moments it holds, and Show moments: each moment as the video, the time and what happened there (four, then Show all). Accept puts the rule into the style, Reject leaves it out; with more than one, Accept all takes every one. A suggestion changes nothing until it is accepted, and the same rule learned again with numbers within 15 % is no news.
- **Talk it through with AI**: AI in the top bar (`Ctrl+J`), or Talk it through with AI above Your rules, opens the AI panel beside the page; Close or `Ctrl+J` puts it away. It is the same agent and conversation as in the editor, but what is sent from here is about the style, not the open video (the field shows Your style instead of the clip and playhead chips). The agent reads the style, talks the change through, and once you agree makes it: your words become one of Your rules, or it changes, accepts or rejects a rule. The page shows the change at once, without reloading: the changed rule lights up for a moment and a toast says “The AI changed your style: …” with Undo. Every change it makes is a version starting with AI:. Only you can go back to the default style.
- **Your rules**: “Tell the AI what to do or not to do” takes a rule in plain words; `Enter` adds it. A rule's row changes it in place (the pencil, `F2` or `Enter` on the focused row; `Enter` saves, `Esc` cancels) or removes it (the bin or `Delete`, with Undo in the toast). They stand in the style under Your rules, before everything learned, and the agent is told they win over everything else. Learning never changes them.
- **Learned rules**: each rule in the style with what it tells the AI and, while learning makes the same choices as the rule, how well the videos learned from back it now. Otherwise the row says why: Change suggested when learning now makes other choices and the change waits under Suggestions; Kept as you chose when learning makes other choices but you rejected that change or reverted the rule, so it stays as it is until Learn again; Not seen lately when learning no longer finds the rule. A rule changed by hand says Changed by you, and learning leaves it as it is. Its … menu Reverts it to how the style had it before its last change or Removes it, both with Undo in a toast.
- **Not learned** (closed at first): rules the creator rejected, removed or reverted, which learning leaves alone, each with Learn again.
- **Learned from**: the videos the suggestions come from, newest first: a recording and the finished video cut from it with their match, or a project with when it was learned. The newest 12 count.
- **Learn my style…** opens a dialog with three tabs; the arrow keys move between them. **Videos** takes 1 to 3 pairs of a recording and the finished video cut from it (Add a pair, the ✕ removes one). Learn compares them one after another on this computer the way `nuzky style learn` does, so the same files give the same rules: each row shows its phase and percentage, then Matched with the share of the finished video's speech found in the recording, or why it was skipped (a finished video not cut from its recording). In Connect your AI, Tab goes through each row's Install guide, Copy and Connect, then the footer; when Connect goes away the focus moves to Done (after Gemini's, to copying agy), Try again keeps it where Connect was, and Skip leaves it on Open a video…. `Esc`, ×, Done or a click beside it close it, and the focus goes back to what opened it. While it is open, the editor's and the home screen's keys wait.

### Tour
Try the sample video (the first start, Get set up) and Take the tour (the home screen's sidebar) open the sample as a new project called "Sample video": a 16:9 talking head video Nuzky ships, "The spotlight effect" by Dianne Glavaš (CC BY 3.0), with its transcript, so captions and cuts need no speech model and work offline. Its tile in Media names the author and the licence in its tooltip, and the credits bar in Audio and Copy credits credit it. It opens like From videos…, and asks first while an agent edits. The editor opens with the AI panel and "Cut the long pauses, then add captions." in its field, and the tour starts.

Each of its eight steps points at one part of the editor: everything else lies under a dim that takes the clicks, so only the part shown and the tour's card respond to the pointer. The card sits beside that part and says "2 of 8", a title, one or two lines and Skip tour, and Next where the step does not move on by itself. The hole follows its part when the window, a pane or the AI panel changes.

1. **Ask the AI to edit**, the AI panel's field: "Tell the AI what you want in your own words. Try this one: press Enter." Sending moves on.
2. **Watch it edit**, the timeline and the AI run in the top bar: "Watch the timeline change. Stop and edit, at the top, lets you take over." It moves on 1.5 s after the edit ends, so the card can be read to the end; stopped, the card says so and offers Next.
3. **One Undo takes it all back**, the edit's card in the AI panel. Left out when the edit changed nothing. Once the edit is undone, by the card's Undo, `Ctrl+Z` or the timeline's Undo, the card says "Undone. The video is back as it was." and offers Redo, which, as `Ctrl+Shift+Z` does, brings the edit back, and the run's card offers Undo again. Next goes on either way.
4. **Play the result**, the preview, on the first caption's frame, so even the still frame shows the edit. The card has the keyboard, so `Space` plays; only Next moves on, and it stops playback. No card of a later step stands over the video.
5. **Fix anything by hand**: the Transcript tab opens with its first word selected and the playhead on it: "The video starts on “But”. Press Delete to cut it." Cutting it, or any words, moves on; Next skips.
6. **Add a song**: Audio's Music opens on the built-in song, its + showing and focused; the song's clip on the timeline moves on.
7. **Fine-tune what's selected**: the song's clip is selected and the inspector's Lower under speech shown: "Tick Lower under speech: the song dips whenever she talks." Ticking it moves on, as does Next. While the step shows, the AI panel stays closed where it would take the inspector's place (under about 1330 px) or float, also when the window or the panel changes meanwhile, and it opens again after.
8. **Export when it's ready**, Export in the top bar, with Open my own video… (as Open a talking head video…). One line always says the tour's AI was a demo. With Claude Code, Codex or ChatGPT on this computer it names the one the panel uses ("Claude Code does it for real on your videos. Next time, type what you want in the AI panel.") and Done ends the tour; without one, Connect your AI takes Done's place, ends the tour and opens Get set up on Connect your AI.

`Esc` or Skip tour end it, and so do opening another project and the home screen. A dialog or menu the tour does not own, such as the launcher (`Ctrl+K`), and the full screen preview on any step but Play the result put the tour aside without ending it: the dim and the card hide until it closes. Moving past Play the result leaves the full screen preview. `Tab` goes round the card and the part shown. The keyboard starts on the part shown where the step's action is there (the field, the transcript, the song's +, Lower under speech), otherwise on the card's Next.

**The tour's AI**: on the sample, while the tour runs, the AI panel is a demo. It runs no agent and needs no Claude Code, Codex, ChatGPT or internet: its header says AI demo instead of the agent, it has no agent, model, past chats, new chat, attachments or picture, and its field takes no typing, only `Enter` or Send. The turn shows the request, then the steps Read the transcript, Cutting long pauses and Adding captions as the project changes, with Working and Stop. Nuzky makes the edit itself as one AI run, "Cut long pauses": the editor locks as for any agent, the cut lands, and after a moment the captions. Then a reply counts what it did ("Done. I cut 17 long pauses, so the video is 3.6 s shorter, and added 80 captions."; it names retakes only when it cut some) and the run's card lists the changes with Undo, as after an agent's turn. Stop in the panel, or Stop and edit in the top bar, ends it as a stopped turn: what it changed stays with its card and Undo. The demo's conversation is never kept with the project; after the tour the panel is the user's own AI again, and the first message starts a new chat. While the scripted turn shows, also after the tour under the user's own AI, a line above it says "Tour demo · no AI ran".

### Launcher
Projects in the editor's top bar, or `Ctrl+K`, opens the launcher over the editor: a search field, Continue (the open project, with the AI run when there is one), the five most recent other projects, and All projects, which goes to the home screen. Below, Empty project starts an empty project in 9:16, 16:9, 1:1 or 4:5, From videos… one of videos, and Open file… opens one from anywhere. It searches like the home screen: projects by name, then Said in your videos, six of each; when there are more, Show all results continues the search on the home screen. The keyboard stays in the search field: `↑`/`↓` move, `Enter` opens (the first result once you type), `Esc` closes and keeps editing; `Tab` reaches the buttons. While an agent edits, the question about its changes shows inside the launcher; `Esc` there cancels it.

### Errors
An error says in plain words what happened and what to do next; an error toast stays until it is dismissed. The engine's coded errors (`READ_ONLY: …`, `EDIT_REJECTED: …` and the like) are translated where they are shown; an error without a known code is shown as it came.

On macOS a file dialog (Export…, Import media, Open file…) that the window darkened for but macOS did not show within 3 s, while the window itself is on the screen, is closed again, as Cancel would, and an error toast says to quit Nuzky, open it again and try once more. macOS 27 once stopped showing them in a window that had been open a while; a newly started Nuzky showed them again.

A job that fails ends with a way on. A failed export shows its reason in the Export dialog with Open logs folder and Retry (Export… when the dialog did not start it); with the dialog closed, a toast offers Details, which opens it. A failed transcript or captions run, also when its speech model could not be downloaded, shows in its panel with Open logs folder under the button that starts it again, and its toast offers Try again, which runs the same request. A failed person model download offers Try again. A job that hit a bug in Nuzky says so and asks to try again and report it if it happens again.

Nuzky keeps a log in the `logs` folder of its data folder: warnings, errors, failed jobs, errors of the interface and panics, with where they happened, and what the person and the AI did: each edit that starts an undo step as its command types with their number and yes-or-no values, undo and redo, AI edits starting and ending, jobs starting and ending, play, pause, importing and opening projects. It holds two files of up to 1 MB each (`nuzky.log` and the older `nuzky.1.log`), written as things happen, so what was done before a crash is still there. It never keeps what someone made or who they are: text between double quotes, curly quotes or backticks, where captions, typed words and project names appear in error messages, is left out, a path keeps only its last part, and tokens, keys, `Authorization` headers and long hexadecimal secrets are hidden. Debug and trace records, where libraries dump whole requests, stay in the terminal. Nothing is ever sent.

A bug report holds the version and the commit it was built from, the system, graphics, memory, the installed speech and face models, whether the Nuzky before closed unexpectedly, the open project's size, length and track and clip counts, whether an AI edit or jobs are running, a line per file of the project with its format read from the file (container, codecs, size, frame rate and whether it varies, rotation, HDR, sound, and the phone and app that recorded it) but never its name, folder, place or title, the last 100 actions and the last 300 lines of the log.

When the window itself hits a bug, it shows "Something went wrong in this window" with Report a bug… and Reload (focused) instead of an empty window. Report a bug… there saves the report and opens the bug form at once, without the dialog, and the card says which file to drag into the issue, with Show in folder; the button is disabled while it saves and once the report is saved. The project lives in the engine, so Reload continues where it was.

When Nuzky ended without quitting, because it crashed, hung and was killed or the computer lost power, the next start says "Nuzky closed unexpectedly. Your project is as it was last saved." with Report a bug…, which opens the Report a bug dialog, after the project opened as described in Starting; an unfinished AI edit still asks what to keep (Interrupted AI edits). Quitting is not a crash, and on Linux and macOS neither is logging out, closing the terminal that runs Nuzky or `kill`. Each running Nuzky holds a lock on its own file in the logs folder, so another open window is never taken for a crash.

### Saving
Every edit is saved about a second later. The top bar shows Saving…, Saved, or a failure with an icon and text. An action that changes nothing, such as a failed import, leaves the status as it was.

Editor shortcuts are ignored on the home screen, in the launcher, while typing in a text field and while a dialog is open. A focused tab bar keeps `←`/`→` (and `Home`/`End`) to switch tabs, so the playhead does not move. The focused transcript keeps `←`/`→`, `Home`/`End`, `Delete`, `Backspace` and `F2` for its words, and there `Ctrl+C`, `Ctrl+X` and `Ctrl+V` do nothing; while a word is being corrected, its field keeps every key.

## Interrupted AI edits

Opening a project with an unfinished AI checkpoint blocks the editor with “An AI edit didn't finish”. “Keep changes” keeps the saved edits. “Restore previous version” restores the checkpoint as one undoable step and offers Undo in a toast. The dialog cannot be dismissed before choosing; a failed recovery keeps it open with the error. While an AI run is open, the top bar shows “AI is editing · <label>” with Stop and edit, and the editing controls are disabled: the inspector, the tools in the left panel, the timeline's edit buttons, track toggles and new transitions, moving and trimming clips, the preview box handles, Ratio and renaming; in the cover editor Pick for me, the frames, the cover's settings and its handles, and choosing a frame on the timeline, which goes back to the agent's frame. Disabled buttons stay reachable by keyboard and give that reason; fields are disabled with it as their tooltip. Selecting, playing and scrubbing still work. An edit tried anyway, by shortcut or drop, is not sent and shows “AI is editing. Stop it to edit yourself.” with Stop and edit. That toast stays until the run ends; dismissed, it comes back with the next refused edit. When a run that changed something ends, “AI edit done” offers Undo for the whole run; for a run of the AI panel's agent, the panel's run card does that instead.
