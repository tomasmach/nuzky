# Edit with Nuzky

Use MCP for every project change. Never write project JSON or overwrite source media. Media, recognition and export stay local; transcripts and inspected frames are visible to your model.

## Cut slips and pauses, then caption

1. `get_state`: check the canvas, assets, duration, speech_layout_key and any open run. If recovery_checkpoint is present, ask whether to keep current edits or restore, then `resolve_recovery` with that choice.
2. `transcribe` with no arguments recognises all heard files lacking stored words. Language defaults to auto and model to the best installed. Poll `job` with pauses until done; the first Czech or English file also downloads the word timing model. `word_times: "measured"` means cuts land on word boundaries even in speech without pauses; with `"estimated"` (no model for the language, or offline) check cuts in connected speech with inspect_frames before keeping them. Explicit asset_ids force recognition again; avoid this unless needed. Transcribe before opening a run.
3. `get_transcript`: read ALL sentences before choosing cuts. For long videos use `detail: "sentences"` with `limit` and pass `next_cursor` as `cursor` until null, keeping the same `range_us` on each page. Request full word-level detail only for the part you cut. Words have zero-based `i`, timeline start_us/end_us, text and probability `p`. Sentences have inclusive `from`/`to` word indices; pauses show after_word and gap_us. Optional range_us filters without renumbering. Transcribe any untranscribed assets before continuing.
4. Start every rough cut with `analyze(kind: "retakes")` right after reading the transcript. It needs no asset_id, answers at once and numbers words exactly as get_transcript (same transcript_key). `groups` are attempts at one restarted sentence, also across clips, with `keep` pointing at the last complete attempt; `fillers` are filler words starting a sentence; `unfinished` are attempts that trail off before a new sentence; `review` lists near-repeats that differ in a negation, a number or a content word, which it never deletes; `suggested_delete` merges every group's `delete` with the fillers and unfinished attempts. Check each group and filler against the text: change a choice only when the text clearly shows another attempt is the one to keep, and decide each `review` pair yourself. Then mark what the analysis cannot know: slips, other false starts and fillers, off-topic detours. Keep the LAST complete take when a sentence restarts. Preserve negations, the argument and its final point. Remove closing calls to action such as “napiš mi do komentáře…” unless the user asks for one. Do not invent a duration target or remove a whole topic just to reach one. The per-asset `fillers` analysis is optional and reads stored words; dictionary matches need context.
5. Delete the reviewed suggested_delete plus your own ranges through `edit_transcript`. Plan with `edit_transcript(transcript_key, delete: [[from,to],...], dry_run: true)`, which needs no open run; alternatively use keep ranges. Word ranges are INCLUSIVE. Use the key from get_transcript. The tool handles source mapping, tight cut padding, edge silence and internal pauses longer than shorten_pauses_us (default 300000). Cuts stay inside clips that carry words, so B-roll is never cut. Never calculate source-to-timeline offsets yourself. Check duration_us.before/after and preview_text. Then `begin_run` and apply with its run_id, dry_run=false, a fresh request_id and the ORIGINAL transcript_key, not the predicted key from the preview. On a save failure, retry identical arguments with that same request_id; the live cut may already be applied. Omitting request_id generates one, so supply it for retryable edits. SPEECH_CHANGED means read the transcript and plan again. Omit delete/keep to shorten pauses only. One rippleDeleteRanges edit respects music tracks kept in place.
6. `build_captions(run_id)` uses current derived words and creates or replaces one Captions track (a text track named `Captions`; titles stay on other text tracks). Default Reel style: size 95, white, regular, black stroke 7.5, no background, Inter; defaults max_words=2/max_chars=15. A single long word stays intact. Captions never span a clip cut; vertical canvases automatically use the safe wrap width. Prefer defaults unless asked otherwise. When the user wants the spoken word highlighted (karaoke, "zvýrazni slovo"), pass `style_preset: "karaoke"` (Reel look, the word being said in yellow) or `"green_box"` (bold white on a dark box, the word in green); give style or style_preset, not both. Correcting a caption word for word (as many words as before) keeps its highlight; adding or removing words turns it off.
7. `inspect_frames(times_us: [...], safe_area: true)` around cuts, caption changes and representative phrases; `inspect_frames(sample: "changes", safe_area: true)` reviews the whole cut with one frame per distinct picture. Shaded margins are outside the IG/TikTok safe area. Check words, readable captions, the face and the ending. Correct transcription mistakes with `correct_words(run_id, transcript_key, request_id, corrections: [{i, text}])`, i from get_transcript and text with its punctuation, never by editing caption text: it fixes the word in the transcript and in the captions that show it, and build_captions keeps it. Never invent speech. After manual caption edits inspect get_state.caption_stats; keep multiword captions within chosen limits. Never abbreviate speech just to fit. Do not add zoom unless asked.
8. `end_run(run_id, action: "keep")`, then export with the preset of the platform: `export_video(path: "reel.mp4", preset: "reels")` for Instagram Reels or TikTok (1080x1920, 30 fps), `"shorts"` for YouTube Shorts (1080x1920), `"youtube_1080p"` or `"youtube_4k"` for a YouTube video (1920x1080, 3840x2160), `"instagram_feed"` for an Instagram post (1080x1350, 30 fps) and `"square"` for a square post (1080x1080, 30 fps). Each writes H.264 High and AAC 48 kHz with the sound levelled to -14 LUFS and true peak at most -1 dBTP in the file (the project keeps its levels). Each needs its canvas ratio (9:16, 16:9, 4:5, 1:1); never change the user's format just to fit a preset. Shorts and YouTube keep the project frame rate, or the fps you pass, up to 60. Other videos: `export_video(path, resolution, fps, quality)` as the user asks; the resolution is the short side. Poll until done and report the real output path, duration and any uncertainty. The output path must be new.

## See the footage

Find where something happens before looking at frames. `activity(range_us?, points?)` returns text only: per step of the timeline `change` (the biggest jump in the picture; 18 or more is a hard cut), `motion` (how much moves within 0.1 s) and `loudness_db` (the mix), and the `peaks` of each with their times. A reel answers at once; a long range may answer with a job: poll it until done or cancel it. Then look only where it matters: `inspect_frames(times_us)` at the change peaks, or `inspect_frames(sample: "changes", range_us)` over a range that interests you. It keeps only frames that look different from the frames kept before, so a talking head costs a frame per shot; pass `next` back as `cursor` while it is not null. Do not sample a talking head evenly by hand.

## Pick a cover frame

For a cover, thumbnail or "náhledovka", run `analyze(kind: "thumbnail_frames")` after the cut: it looks at the picture as exported, with colours and zooms but without text, as a job; poll it. Pass `params: {format: "9:16"}` for a Reels or TikTok cover and `"16:9"` for a YouTube thumbnail; the canvas shape is the default. `candidates` (6-8, best first, at least 2 s apart) carry `parts` that say why: eyes_open, mouth (not mid-word), facing the camera, smile against this face's usual look, sharpness, exposure and framing for each format, plus `crops` for each format. Look at the top ones with `inspect_frames(times_us)` and choose; the score guides, it does not decide. To put text behind the person, `segment_subject(time_us)` returns the subject's mask as a grayscale PNG in the cache with its box and share of the frame; it takes several seconds and the same frame comes from the cache next time. MODEL_MISSING: tell the user to install the models with `nuzky vision-models`; never download them yourself. VISION_UNAVAILABLE: covers cannot run on this computer; say so and carry on with the edit.

The project keeps one cover (`cover_9x16`, 1080×1920 for Reels, TikTok and Shorts) and one YouTube thumbnail (`youtube_16x9`, 1280×720); `get_state.thumbnails` shows them. The `thumbnail` prompt makes both. Inside a run, `apply_edits` `{"type":"setThumbnail","thumbnail":{format, timeUs, frame, background, texts, outline}}` replaces the one of its format and `removeThumbnail {format}` drops it. From the bottom up it draws the background, texts with `behind: true`, the person cut out of the frame at `timeUs` by its mask, the outline around them and the other texts. `frame` is a transform as for clips: scale 1 fits the whole frame inside the thumbnail, x and y move its centre by fractions of the thumbnail. `background` {picture, blur 0–1, dim 0–1, color}: picture true shows the frame behind the person, enlarged about its centre until it covers the thumbnail, false only the colour. `outline` {color, width up to 100 px}. Texts are {text, style, transform, behind} with sizes in thumbnail pixels; the text presets are in the `thumbnail` prompt. Text behind the person, an outline or a background that is blurred, dimmed or a colour need the mask of the person; inspect_thumbnail starts it as a job and says MASK_NOT_READY until it is made. For a cover from a vertical video keep the frame as it is and put big text behind the head; for YouTube from a vertical video put the person on a third with a blurred or coloured background and the hook beside them. `inspect_thumbnail(format, safe_zones: true)` draws it as the file will be, with the share of each text behind the person that they hide (`hidden`, 0.05–0.3 reads well), and tints what the Reels interface, the 3:4 profile grid and YouTube's duration badge cover. `export_thumbnail(format, path)` writes a new .png or .jpg as a job; use .jpg for YouTube, which stays under its 2 MB limit. Cuts made later move `timeUs` with the picture.

## Reels from a long video

When the user wants several reels from one long recording (a podcast, a talk, a stream), find the moments, let them choose, then make each a project of its own.

1. Transcribe as above, then read the whole video with `get_transcript(detail: "sentences", limit: 100)`, passing `next_cursor` back until it is null. Look for moments that stand alone: they open with a line that makes someone stay (a question, a bold claim, a surprise), keep to one idea, end on its payoff and need nothing said before them.
2. In a run, `propose_reels(transcript_key, candidates: [{from, to, title, why, score}])` with the best 3 to 10, each from the first word of a sentence to the last word of one and 15 to 60 s once cut. The tool measures each and refuses one that is too long, overlaps another or starts or ends mid-sentence; fix that one and propose again. It does not compare with reels already made, so read get_state's `reel_candidates` and do not propose their moments again. Write title and why in the user's language; why says in a sentence what makes it work alone. Proposing again replaces what was proposed before; made reels stay. Then `end_run` keep and ask the user which to make, or let them pick in the app.
3. In a run, `make_reels(ids)` makes the picked ones (status `picked` in get_state's `reel_candidates`), or the ones the user named. Each becomes a new project beside this one with only its words on a 9:16 canvas: `canvas: "crop"` (default) fills it with the middle of the picture, `"blur"` shows the whole picture over a blurred copy, better when the speaker is off centre or several people share the frame. The long video stays as it was. Report the paths.
4. Each reel is its own project, and this server edits only this one. Captions, a cover and the export happen in the reel, with the steps above, once the user opens it in Nuzky or through an MCP server started on its path (`nuzky mcp --project <path> --allow-write`).

## Make it dynamic, only when asked

For “rozhýbej to, občas zoom, pomalý nájezd”, or when the creator's style asks for zoom, read the story first. Keep it subtle, not on every sentence. For 1.15–1.3 punch-ins on key claims or punchlines run `analyze(kind: "emphasis")` after the cuts: it answers at once with zooms {from,to,start_us,end_us,text,score,scale} on get_transcript's word numbers and transcript_key, scored by loudness, exclamation and the opening hook, at least 5 s apart and about one per 12 s. AUDIO_NOT_READY names a job preparing the sound; poll it, then analyze again. Read each against the text: drop one that is not a claim or punchline, add a sentence it missed, keep the count low. Apply them inside a run with `apply_zooms(run_id, transcript_key, zooms: [{from,to,scale}], request_id)`: one undo step, the picture changes in the silence between words and the sound stays as it was; clips with keyframes are left alone and listed in skipped. For a slow push-in of 6–10 % over one sentence when building up, a pull-out at a reveal or turn, or a Ken Burns move on a photo, call `apply_motion(run_id, kind, strength, range_us or clip_id, request_id)` inside a run: kind `pushIn`, `pullOut` or `kenBurns`, strength 0.06, 0.1 or 0.15. `range_us` [start_us of the sentence's first word, end_us of its last] from get_transcript moves the main-track clips under it as one continuous move; `clip_id` moves one video or image clip, also on an overlay track, over its whole length or the part inside `range_us`. It writes two keyframes per clip on a smooth curve from the clip's own framing, centred on the middle of the safe area on vertical canvases so the face stays clear of the apps' buttons; nothing to compute. Clips that already have keyframes are left alone and listed in skipped. A constant base zoom around 1.1 is updateClip transform. Read nuzky://schema for exact edit fields. A complete transform is {"x":0,"y":0,"scale":1.2,"rotation":0,"opacity":1}. Inspect frames with safe_area=true after changes.

## B-roll and picture in picture

To show a screenshot, a second camera or other B-roll while the speaker says something, find the words in get_transcript and use the timeline start_us of the first word and end_us of the last. Inside a run, `apply_edits` with `{"type":"addPictureInPicture","assetId":…,"startUs":start,"durationUs":end-start}` puts the video or image in a small window with rounded corners and a soft shadow in the top right corner of the safe area, on an overlay track above the speaker; text cuts never cut it. For B-roll over the whole frame, follow with updateClip `transform` {"x":0,"y":0,"scale":1,"rotation":0,"opacity":1} and `shape` {}. updateClip `shape` sets `radius` (0–1 of half the shorter side), `borderWidth` (canvas px), `borderColor` and `shadow` (0–1); `transform.crop` {left,top,right,bottom} cuts those shares off the picture as shown, without moving the rest, and keyframes animate it. For a round camera bubble set radius 1 and crop the longer side so a square is left, e.g. left and right 0.21875 of a 16:9 video. Inspect frames with safe_area=true after.

## Music and sound effects

When the user asks for music or a sound effect (a whoosh on a cut, a pop on a caption), find it with `search_sounds(query, kind: "music" | "effect")`: short English words such as "whoosh", "upbeat" or "cinematic". Only CC0 and CC BY ever come back, so every result is fine in a monetized video. Effects start with the ones built into Nuzky, which work offline; online results follow. Inside a run, `add_sound(run_id, id, at_us)` downloads the sound when needed and places it on an audio track at that time. Put a whoosh slightly before a cut and music from 0, then duck it under speech. When `needs_credit` is true, tell the user the video's description must credit it: the Audio panel's Copy credits and the `.credits.txt` written beside the export have the text. Offline, online_error says so; use the built-in effects or tell the user.

## Runs and low-level edits

All timeline times are integer microseconds; time ranges are half-open [start,end). Word-index ranges above are inclusive. apply_edits takes camelCase EditCmd fields, a fresh request_id per batch, and optional expected_revision/expected_speech_layout_key from get_state. edit_transcript instead uses get_transcript.transcript_key, which also guards against re-recognition. Retry identical content with the same request_id after a save failure; the live edit may already be applied. The main track is magnetic. One run is one Undo; end_run(discard) restores its start. Runs auto-keep after two idle minutes, so plan before opening one. A stopped run returns RUN_STOPPED; never resume it. undo_run works only for the last history entry in this session. To go back further, also to an earlier session, `list_history` lists the kept versions (each kept run, the user's steps, undos and restores) with index, label and short hash, and `undo_to(index)` restores one as a new step outside a run, so the versions after it stay. Ask the user before restoring over their own edits. Never change the user's selection.

## The creator's style

The style (`get_style`, also `nuzky://style`) is how this creator edits all their videos; its rules override this guide, and the rules under Your rules are the creator's own words and win over everything. When the user talks about how you should edit in general rather than this video ("never cut my sign-off", "the pauses feel too short"), or writes from the Your style page, read `get_style`, talk it through, and only once they agree make the change with `change_style`: what they tell you goes in their words as `setOwn {text}`; changing how a learned rule reads is `setText` with the whole EDIT.md and `baseVersion` from `get_style`; suggestions are theirs to `accept` or `reject`. Say in a sentence what changed; the app shows it at once and the user can undo it there. Going back to the default style is only for the user, in the app.

When a decision is the user's to make (which take to keep, tight or loose cuts, which caption style) and you run as Nuzky's AI panel, call `suggest_options(question, options: [{label, detail}])` with 2–6 short choices as the last thing in your turn and end the turn; the panel shows them as buttons and the label picked comes back as the user's next message. In a terminal, ask in plain text instead. Decide yourself whatever the guide or the creator's style already answers.

For speech recorded with audible room noise, hum or harsh s sounds, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"cleanVoice":true}` on its clips with sound (high-pass, gentle denoise, de-ess; not for music); export waits for the cleaned sound.

To blur or replace what is behind a talking head, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"background":{"type":"blur","strength":0.5}}` on every clip of it, or `{"type":"color","color":"#1c1c1e"}`, `{"type":"image","assetId":…}` of an imported image, `{"type":"none"}` to undo; a colour with alpha 00 leaves only the person over the tracks below. The person is found once per file in the background: inspect_frames answers MATTE_NOT_READY with a job to poll, export_video does it itself, and MODEL_MISSING means the user installs the models with `nuzky vision-models`. It suits one person filling much of the frame; check the edges with inspect_frames.

To speed up a talking head, 1.1–1.25 is common: `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"speed":1.2}`. A clip moved off 1x keeps the pitch of its voice (`keepPitch` turns on); add `"keepPitch":false` only when the user wants the voice higher or lower with the speed. Captions and word times follow the speed either way.

For music under speech, set `{"type":"updateClip","clipId":…,"duckDb":12}` on the music clip instead of lowering its volume by hand: it goes 12 dB down while a video's own sound has speech and comes back in the pauses, the same in playback and export. Speech is any sound in video clips above -40 dBFS, never an imported audio file. 6 is gentle, 18 strong, up to 40; 0 turns it off.
